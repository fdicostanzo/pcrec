B. WHERE IS `(?i)` READ -- at the GROUP or at the BACKREFERENCE?
==============================================================================
pattern                    subj   libpcre2       cell
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
^(?i:(a))\1$               'aA'   no match       (?i) scoped to the GROUP only
^(?i:(a))\1$               'aa'   (0, 2)           ... same, exact-case subject
^(a)(?i)\1$                'aA'   (0, 2)         (?i) turned on AFTER the group
^(?i)(a)(?-i)\1$           'aA'   no match       (?i) on at the group, OFF at the backref
^(?i)(a)(?-i)\1$           'aa'   (0, 2)           ... same, exact-case subject
^(a)(?i:\1)$               'aA'   (0, 2)         (?i) scoped to the BACKREF only
^(?i)(a)\1$                'aA'   (0, 2)         (?i) on at both
^((?i)a)\1$                'aA'   no match       (?i) inside the group, backref outside
^((?i)a)\1$                'Aa'   no match         ... other order

If the (?i)-scoped-to-the-BACKREF rows match and the
(?i)-scoped-to-the-GROUP rows do not, the compare's caselessness
is a property of the BACKREFERENCE NODE -- a parse-resolved field,
D62's rule, exactly as `Ast.multiline` is for `$`.

C. LENGTH. Is the caseless compare length-preserving in 8-bit?
==============================================================================

----------------------------------------------------------------------

'(?i)^(\\xdf)\\1$'     'ßss'          no match
'(?i)^(\\xe0)\\1$'     'àÀ'           no match

In the 8-bit NON-UTF build every fold pair is one byte to one
byte, so the compare cannot change length. The rows above are the
EVIDENCE for that, and the sharp-s row is the counterexample a
UTF-8 backend would have to handle and this one does not.
