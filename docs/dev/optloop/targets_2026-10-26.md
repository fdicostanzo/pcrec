## How to read the numbers

- **Ratio** is pcrec time / peer time: x2.4 means pcrec takes 2.4 times as long. "Best non-pcrec" is the fastest of
  jit, interp, rust, re2, onig, tre that has a measured, expectation-passing cell on the same row; it is NAMED in
  every cell. **Vectorscan block-nosom is excluded everywhere** (boolean grain, stops at the first match; bench
  caveat 3). I did not re-admit it on any no-match-only row: no row of this table is known to be all-no-match. On
  capability `json-constant` the vectorscan set-grain sum (189.7 us) is below rust's (226.3 us), so it would be the
  fastest there if admitted; nothing else in this table changes.
- **Set grain sums subjects.** A set-grain `large` cell is the sum over the set's large subjects (checked: bounded
  `ctx-lazy-64` set-grain 102,642 ns = the five subject cells). So a "set-grain loss" is time-weighted toward the
  largest subject. Every row below says how the sum splits; a **DOMINATED-SET** flag is raised when ONE subject is
  more than 90% of the sum. Result over all 1,093 set-grain rows: **no target row has a single subject above 90%**.
  The closest are capability `trim-nested-star` (t-trim-match-60k 88%) and the hazard rows where TWO subjects
  (a 60 KiB subject and its 16 KiB near-miss twin) are ~100% (named in row T3).
- **Ratio source** per row: **direct (B)** = pcrec 6b2dbd3e and the engine in the same window B, same box, same
  pin; the 9ae59f5d4 ratio is then **chained** = direct(B) x (pcrec@9ae59f5d4 / pcrec@6b2dbd3e, window A). For
  **capability** there is no pcrec-auto vs engine comparison inside window B (the auto-caps and auto-nocaps records
  are `inconclusive-spread`: 137 and 139 `not_ranked` rows; only vm-caps is measured there), so capability rows are
  **cross-window**: pcrec auto from window A (9ae59f5d4 and 6b2dbd3e) against window B's engine records, joined on
  the same (pattern, subject); all 11 subjects agree per pattern.
- **Uncertainty of a ratio.** Peer drift across windows is small (bench I-147: median 0.998; jit p5-p95 0.935-1.034;
  interp 0.838-1.034). Measured here on pcrec itself, same pin, window A vs window B (large cells): median 1.00,
  p5-p95 0.96-1.04 (syntax, n=80), 0.97-1.01 (loglines), 0.99-1.02 (litrun), 0.94-1.02 (utf8), 0.87-1.03 (bounded);
  sentinel short cells (~100-1000 ns): pcrec median 0.94, jit 0.88-1.17. Read every ratio as +-5%; a chained ratio
  +-7%. Nothing ranked below is within that of x1.3.
- **pin delta** = pcrec 9ae59f5d4 / 6b2dbd3e on pcrec's own cell, window A, auto-caps. `n/a` = the row is not in
  window A (all of `altwide`, and the subset of bounded/syntax/utf8 rows window A did not carry); the 9ae59f5d4
  ratio is then taken equal to the 6b2dbd3e one, uncertainty as above.
- **Class caveat.** Every engine is a `-caps` config; pcrec auto-caps is compared to them (caps to caps). pcrec
  auto-nocaps is within 1% of auto-caps on every cell in the loss lists below, so the choice does not change a rank.
- **Zen 4 vs Zen 1 are not comparable in absolute ns**; rev 0's Zen 1 ratios are used only in the "what changed"
  section. Window B runs pcrec 6b2dbd3e; all losses were checked against window A, where 9ae59f5d4 is within the
  null band of 6b2dbd3e on every target except the movers in section 2.

Source tags in cells: **B** direct window-B; **A->B** cross-window (capability); **chain** = B x pin delta.

| `email-owasp` `^[a-zA-Z0-9_+&*-]+(...)@...$` | t-evil-match-60k 79%, t-evil-nearmiss-16k 21% | 61,440 B of **random lowercase letters** / first 16 KiB + `!` | 23.2 us, 6.17 us (0.378 ns/B both) | 0.375 us, 0.077 us | 1.46 us, 0.41 us | 68.3 us, 18.2 us | pcrec 4-7 ns vs jit 22-29 ns (x4-6 faster) |

So: **iso8601's losing subjects are long whitespace-class (`\s`, a union of 6 bytes) runs; email-owasp's are long
runs of letters (the class `[a-zA-Z0-9_+&*-]`, a union of several byte classes once the later `[a-zA-Z]{2,}` splits it).**
On this box jit loses nothing on either (x4-6 faster than jit on ordinary subjects, as Zen 1 reported x4-7). Zen 1's
x15-23 vs jit was the same two-subject effect; on Zen 4 it is x22.2 / x7.5. The same pattern holds for `trim-nested-star`
(t-trim-match-60k 88%), `evil-alt-nested` and `pwd-strength-chain`. Rust is as slow as pcrec on iso8601 (68.2 us, a full
scan), so the engine to beat there is jit/vm-caps, not rust.

The forced-vm route does the same 60 KB in 0.37 us (164 B/ns): 183x faster than auto on iso8601, 62x on email-owasp,
and jit's 1.46 us is also a reject, not a scan. Both subjects contain no `-` (iso8601's required separator) / no `@`
(email's), which is how vm and jit can reject without walking the run: **hypothesis, not read from code**
(a required-byte presence check). This is the observation that makes a pure scan-edge fix insufficient (section 4).
