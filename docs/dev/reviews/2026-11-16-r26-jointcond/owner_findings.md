### OWNER-5 — J8's lift is a rule keyed on a glossary word, and `padded`'s own ruled body cannot take a tuple; the general form is distribution over `|`, a sibling of "an array of X is an X" (CONFIRMED by quote; severity design; effort S-M)
- Where: J8 (`:138-140`), §4A.4 producer (6) (`:683-684`: "a FILTER MODIFIER applied to one (`padded(t)`), acting on every component"), item 3 table (`:271-272`: "`any_case`, `quoted` and other modifiers: the same"), §3 row (`:170`, citing `padded(rx) := /0*${rx}/`).
- The draft's `padded` is a PLAIN MACRO whose body puts its argument in an rx hole (`predicates_draft.md:1281`, RULED). The note's own definition makes "a tuple where an arity-1 set is required: an rx HOLE (`/${t}/`)" an ERROR (`:696`). Read through its body, `padded((9, 0))` is therefore an arity error. J8 overrides the body by a rule that applies to "a modifier", which is a glossary word (`predicates_draft.md:780`: "a transform that changes WHAT THE FILTER READS"), not a fact the compiler can see. The note never says which macros lift. Example: a user's `zp(x) := /0*${x}/`, identical to `padded`. Does it lift, or is it an arity error? And `length`, `left(n, S)`, `month_numbered`?
- By his criteria this is a SPECIAL CASE (memory: general mechanisms, not special cases) and against "simplifying semantics" (CF 11:35). The general form, which I believe matches his "do everything to both": a macro whose result DISTRIBUTES OVER `|` in its argument (f(A | B) = f(A) | f(B); every hole-substitution body and every pullback does) is applied to a k-ary set R as the union, over R's tuples, of f(t1) x ... x f(tk). This is the same law as "an array of X is an X" (CF 2026-11-14 00:10, 11-16 00:08): an operation on a set is its operation on the members. Macros that do not distribute (a body with `!x`, or `x` used twice) stay arity errors with the existing hint. The rule is checkable from the body, so `padded` and `zp` behave the same, and `padded` is no longer special.
- UNLOCK (sub-part 2a): the parked per-part transform (`predicates_future.md` item 2) becomes the same law with a different map per component, (f1 x f2). It needs no new mechanism beyond a way to NAME the per-component maps. That naming edges toward the "array of macros" he declined at 00:08, so the TRIGGER stays item 2's own. Item 2 should record that the mechanism is J8's law, not a new one.
- Blast radius: J8, §4A.4 (6), item 3 table, rev 20 §2.7 (the modifier section) and the §5.4 hole row.

### OWNER-7 — The general form the note does not state: a match is a TABLE of passes and the joint condition is a ROW predicate; it explains J7 and unlocks two things (SUSPECTED as framing; severity design; effort S to state)
- Sub-part 2a. In the general form, the passes of the pairing loop are ROWS. A label set once outside the loop is a column constant over the rows (the parent row, as in a join). `((p1..pk): R)` selects rows whose (p1..pk) is in R. The plain condition asks that ALL rows qualify, and `some`/`count` are the other quantifiers (§4.3). Under this reading the record is a product predicate (J9), per-pass pairing is "the rows of the deepest loop" (pairing-loop), and sibling loops are "two tables with no join key" (the error). Zero passes are "a parent row with no child rows": the corners of OWNER-2 are exactly SQL's LEFT JOIN with NULL, where `ALL` over zero rows is true and the parent's columns are still tested. The note cites SQL only for row-value comparison and `IN` (`:291`, `:1541`), not for this. Stating it would give J7 a well-known reference model, and the owner a one-line account of loops.
- UNLOCK U1, sibling loops paired by POSITION (the i-th h with the i-th k). Today it is an error. Value: parallel lists (`names=a,b,c;values=1,2,3`, a header row against data rows). Level: pairing by position needs counting, not regular in general, so the error is right today. TRIGGER: a real example of parallel lists with a per-pair constraint. Not in `predicates_future.md`.
- UNLOCK U2, a predicate over SUCCESSIVE rows (each time after the previous: a sorted list). Value: logs and schedules. Level: regular only at bounded width (finite classes, §4.6-style pricing). TRIGGER: a sortedness problem in a real corpus. Not in `predicates_future.md`.
