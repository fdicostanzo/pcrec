## Findings

### R3-1: normalising against I (the F11 fix) plus "largest h among equals" (F10) discards every head that wins in some cells and ties in the rest. Severity: BLOCKS POSTING (plan A default)

- W(h) = max over cells of t(H(h))/t(I). Any cell where H(h) ties I pins W(h) at about 1. Rev 3's own text says miss-heavy candidates die at depth 0-1, where every H(h) with h >= 2 executes like I.
- Within C1-C2 lengths, H(64) IS I, so W(64) is A/A, about 1.
- So a head that beats I in hit-dense and ties it elsewhere has W of about 1. It lands in E with 64, and the largest-h tie-break picks 64.
- Drill (requested): H(8) is best in one cell and H(32) in another. Cells are c1 (k=24, hit-dense), c2 (k=60, hit-dense) and c3 (miss-heavy).
  - H(8) = (0.80, 0.95, 1.00) and H(32) = (1.00 [it is I at k=24], 0.85, 1.00), so W(8) = W(32) = W(64) = 1.00.
  - E holds {8, 16, ..., 64}, so h\* = 64. Both measured wins are dropped.
  - R2 files only cells where I beats H(h\*), so the wins are FILED NOWHERE either.
- Under rev 2's per-cell-best normaliser, H(8) would have won here. The defect is fix-induced: rev 3 §3 "Why each choice" ¶1 and r22 F11's "Normalise against a FIXED, always-timed arm".
- Effect: a shorter head ships only if it beats I beyond `f` in EVERY regime and layer. The stability-trade text (§3, line ~450: "faster beyond the noise in every regime") says so. The owner question "forms-now-or-next" ("the default he asked for ships") does not.
- Disposition, FIX-NOW (text): add a dominance step before the tie-break. Remove from E every h that another member of E dominates: the other is faster beyond `f` in at least one cell and no slower beyond `f` in any cell. Then take the largest survivor.
  - In this drill: H(8) and H(32) each dominate 64. Neither dominates the other, so the survivors are {8, 32} and h\* = 32.
  - In the predicted tie nothing dominates, so h\* = 64 is unchanged.
  - Alternatively, file every such win, in a new R-row beside R2, as evidence for the owner. Then re-run drills (i)-(viii) and state the result.

### R3-2: "a never-picked value cannot move the pick" is false; only a LOSING value cannot. Severity: FIX BEFORE POSTING (a false claim in the rationale)

- E's threshold is `(1 + f) x min_g W(g)`, so it still depends on the grid's best value.
- Drill: f = 0.025, W(8) = 0.97, W(16) = 0.99, W(24..64) = 1.00.
  - min W = 0.97 and the threshold is 0.994, so E = {8, 16} and h\* = 16.
  - Add h = 4 with W = 0.95. The threshold becomes 0.974, so E = {4, 8} and h\* = 8.
  - h = 4 is never picked, but the pick moved from 16 to 8.
- Drill (vii) only exercises a value that LOSES.
- Disposition, FIX-NOW (text): say "a value that is not the minimum cannot move E". Or accept the dependence as inherent to "within f of the best".

### R3-3: on any tie the shipped h is the grid's CHOSEN top (64), but rev 3 labels it MEASURED. Severity: FIX BEFORE POSTING (honest owner framing; Goal 3)

- Under F10 every tie resolves to max(grid) = 64. The grid is "UNMEASURED: CHOSEN" (§3 "The grid").
- Inside C1-C2, H(64) is I (§3's own note), so no R1 cell ever distinguishes 64 from any larger head. Loops past 64, the stage-2 purpose, are timed only by C3, whose R5 "never re-picks".
- Goal 3 ("every shipped h ... MEASURED or DERIVED") and the drill summary ("no UNMEASURED h ships") overstate this.
- Disposition, FIX-NOW (text):
  - Label a tie outcome "h = grid top (64, CHOSEN), on a measured tie of every finite head".
  - State that the length at which loops begin, 64, is the grid's choice, checked only by R5.
  - Optionally let R5's loss against I or L be the trigger that extends the grid past 64.
- R3-1's fix lowers how often this happens, but does not remove it.
