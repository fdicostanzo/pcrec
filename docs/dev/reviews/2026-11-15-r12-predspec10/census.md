### CENSUS-1 (CONFIRMED; severity design; effort S-M; blast radius §2.6, §4.3, §3.2, §5.4 `$` row, §13 C17/O50/O56): `$` on a repeated label yields label-free captured strings, yet the owner's-example lines read labels inside them
- Draft text. §0.3 draft:369: "**captured string** | `$x`, the text a label captured in this match (a one-string rx)"; §11 draft:1930-1932: "CAPTURED STRINGS (`$x`, a one-string set) ... are CONTEXT-FREE"; §2.1 KIND TABLE draft:495 puts "captured string `$x`" under rx; §5.4 `$` row draft:1484: "the CAPTURED STRINGS of the whole path ... ONE ENTRY PER ITERATION"; and "`$a.b.c` reads the path `a.b.c` ... never the field `index` of `$mname`".
- Against it. §2.6 draft:781-782, 787-790: "`$m.hit[2]` is its entry 2, `.g` a label inside that entry, and `& (g: "x")` distributes over the entries and keeps the passing ones with their original positions"; §4.3 draft:1140-1142 the same ("`$m.hit[2].g` (a label inside it ...)", "`$m.hit & (g: "x")`: `&` distributes over `$m.hit`'s entries and keeps iterations 1 and 3"); §13 O50 (a) draft:2673-2674 and C17 draft:2422-2424 repeat it.
- Rule applied: an entry of `$m.hit` is a captured string = a one-string rx with NO labelled group (§0.3, §11, kind table). A condition `(g: "x")` laid against it by `&` looks g up in its operand (§4.1 draft:1029-1030: "an unknown label is an error listing those available"), so `$m.hit & (g: "x")` is that ERROR, not "keeps iterations 1 and 3". Likewise `.g` after `$m.hit[2]` is a field of a label-free string's map interface (empty), so `($m.hit[2]).g` has nothing to name; the only reading the `$` row admits is the WHOLE PATH `m.hit[2].g` (iteration 2's g text), which is what draft:1484 says and what draft:789 ("`.g` a label inside that entry") contradicts: the doc gives the one spelling both parses P6-6 separated.
- Two meanings: `$` (and "captured string") = label-free text in §0.3/§11/§2.1/§4.1/§5.4; = an iteration that still carries its labels and positions in §2.6/§4.3/§13. Also "keeps ... their original positions" is a structural effect of `&` that §3.2 tool 2 does not state and §13 C17 (draft:2418-2421) lists as UNSTATED, while C17's own last sentence says the example "no longer depends on C17".
- Counter-evidence weighed: §7 draft:1648-1650 gives a precedent of a "one-string rx (context-free) whose labels stay reachable by the map interface" (a `records` ITEM, `r.word`). So "a one-string rx WITH labels" exists for items, but the `$` row, §0.3 and §11 never say a CAPTURED string carries labels or positions; the two parses of `$m.hit[2].g` remain either way.
- Fix shape: either (a) the selection is written over the ITERATIONS, not over `$` (e.g. a group-question/`where`-style selection of the label m.hit, the strings read out by `$` only after selecting), or (b) state that `$` on a repeated label yields per-iteration MATCH RECORDS (with labels and positions), which is a second meaning of `$` and of "captured string" and must replace §0.3/§11's "one-string, context-free". Only the owner's intended behaviour (his `d & (g: x)`, CF:880-887) is fixed; the spelling is the document's (LEAN, FIXA-3).

### CENSUS-2 (CONFIRMED; severity design/doc; effort S; blast radius §11 THE MARKS INSIDE `/.../`, §5.4 preamble, §7, G3): rev 10's "EXACTLY TWO" exceptions inside `/.../` omit the third, `(?&name)` naming an expression binding
- Draft text (rev 10 addition). §11 draft:1861-1863: "A literal means what PCRE means EXCEPT for exactly two marks, which are the predicate language's two additions to rx text"; draft:1880: "So C2's "a literal means EXACTLY what PCRE means" (CF:577) holds for every other mark"; §5.4 draft:1453-1455: "with exactly TWO stated exceptions".
- Against it. §11 draft:1853-1854: "Crossings: rx -> expression by `${...}`, and by `(?&name)` naming the pattern binding whose own literal it sits in"; §7 draft:1642-1644: `balanced := /\((?:[^()]|(?&balanced))*\)/`, "the one crossing from rx text to the expression language other than `${}`".
- Oracle: `python3 -I R/oracle.py search '\((?:[^()]|(?&balanced))*\)' '(a(b))'` -> `compile error at 14: reference to non-existent subpattern`. With a real group, `'(?<balanced>\((?:[^()]|(?&balanced))*\))'` -> `[(0, 6), (0, 6)]`. So inside `/.../` `(?&balanced)` does NOT have PCRE's meaning (PCRE rejects the literal): it names the expression-language BINDING. That is a third non-PCRE reading of rx text, which the "exactly two" sentence (and its G3 consequence "holds for every other mark") contradicts.
- Fix shape: list `(?&name)` (naming the binding the literal belongs to) as the third stated exception in §11's list and the §5.4 preamble, or respell the self-recursion so the literal is PCRE-valid on its own (e.g. a named group around the body), which needs no exception. Its status is LEAN (CF:113 is a manager lean, draft:1976), so either is the manager's call.

### CENSUS-3 (CONFIRMED; severity design; effort S-M; blast radius §11 `&` bullet, §5.4 `&` row, G3, §7 self-recursion, §13 O60): with the rx-`&` switch ON, "`&` everywhere" swallows PCRE's own `(?&name)`, and its `\&` rewrite is a PCRE syntax error
- Draft text (rev 10 addition). §11 draft:1874-1875: "ON: `&` is the INTERSECTION operator everywhere in that rx text ... a literal ampersand is `\&`"; draft:1877-1878 "There is NO position-dependent reading"; §5.4 `&` row draft:1477: "in rx text ONLY with the rx-`&` switch ON, then everywhere in that text"; G3 draft:187: "`&` only with the switch ON (then the transfer writes `\&`; 165 corpus pattern lines contain `&`)". Owner's words: "fully yes or no" (CF:979); record "No position-dependent meaning" (CF:984).
- Against it. PCRE's subroutine call `(?&name)` spells `&` as part of a group opener, not as a literal. The draft's own self-recursion crossing uses it: §7 draft:1642 `balanced := /\((?:[^()]|(?&balanced))*\)/`, §11 draft:1853.
- Counts (frozen-tree corpus, working tree `tests/`): `grep -rhE '^pattern .*&' --include='*.rxt' tests | wc -l` = 165 (holds); `grep -rhE '^pattern .*\(\?&' ... | wc -l` = 154: 154 of the 165 `&` lines are `(?&name)` calls, not literal ampersands. A further 5 have `&` inside a character class (RFC 5322 atext, `[a-z0-9!#$%&'*+/=?^_`{|}~-]`).
- Oracle: `python3 -I R/oracle.py search '(?<a>x)(?\&a)' 'xx'` -> `compile error at 9: unrecognized character after (? or (?-`. So the stated G3 rewrite (`&` -> `\&`) turns every `(?&name)` into a PCRE compile error, and under ON "everywhere" `(?&balanced)` would parse as an intersection, breaking §7's example in ON mode. (In a class, `[a\&b]` matches `&`: `[(0, 1)]`, so the class case is only unstated, not broken.)
- The tension with the ruling: exempting `(?&` (and `(?P>`-style or class positions) is exactly a position-dependent reading, which CF:984 excludes; not exempting it makes subroutine calls unwritable with the switch ON. Neither §11 nor §13 O60 names the case. Fix shape: state it in O60 as a question for the owner (e.g. `(?&` is one PCRE TOKEN, not the `&` mark, so "everywhere" means every `&` that is not part of a PCRE token: classes and `\Q...\E` would then also need saying), and correct G3's sentence: the ON transfer rewrites only the 11 non-`(?&` lines.

### CENSUS-4 (CONFIRMED; severity doc; effort S; blast radius §11 `${` bullet): "the exception takes no matchable PCRE meaning away" is false in a class and in `\Q...\E`
- Draft text (rev 10 addition). §11 draft:1864-1868: "`${` ALWAYS OPENS A HOLE inside rx text ... in every position (an atom, a class, a quantifier, a group name ...) ... In PCRE `${` is the anchor `$` then a literal `{` ... which can NEVER match ... and `${2}` is a PCRE compile error. So the exception takes no matchable PCRE meaning away."
- Oracle: `search '[${]x' '{x'` -> `[(0, 2)]` (in a class `$` and `{` are literals and match); `search '\Q${\E' 'a${'` -> `[(1, 3)]`. The pattern-position claims hold (`x${num}` compiles and never matches; `x${0}y`, `x${0,1}y`, `x${,2}y` and `x${2}` are all `quantifier does not follow a repeatable item`).
- So in two of the positions the bullet itself lists (a class; `\Q...\E`), `${` HAS a matchable PCRE meaning that the hole reading takes away. The G3 rewrite the bullet prescribes (`$\{`, `$\E\{\Q`) handles it, so no answer moves for a transferred pattern; the false sentence is the justification a reader is given. Fix: restrict the "can never match" sentence to pattern positions and say that in a class or `\Q...\E` the literal pair is rewritten (as already prescribed).

### CENSUS-5 (CONFIRMED by rule application; severity design (G0 look-alike); effort S; blast radius §2.3, §11 HOLES, §13 C3/O60): the "guard" `(\d+)&${ <256 }` is atom-scoped in its prose but whole-concatenation-scoped by the stated precedence
- Draft text. §2.3 draft:573-575: "a guard is written with `&` before an rx hole, `(\d+)&${ <256 }` with the rx-`&` switch ON"; §11 draft:1943-1945: "a GUARD is written with an explicit `&` ... `(\d+)&${ <256 }` ...; a hole after an atom WITHOUT `&` is a FOLLOWING item"; §13 O60 (B) draft:2853: "`(\d+)&${ <256 }` works as written"; C3 draft:2393.
- Rule. §11 draft:1874-1875 (rev 10): ON, `&` has "D196's semantics and precedence (concatenation, then `&`, then `|`)"; §2.2 draft:555 the same.
- Applied: alone, `/(\d+)&${<256}/` = `(\d+)` ∩ {0..255}: the guard. Inside any longer literal, e.g. `/port=(\d+)&${<256}/` or `/(\d+)&${<256}ms/`, concatenation binds first, so it is `/port=(\d+)/ & <256` (resp. `/(\d+)/ & /${<256}ms/`): EMPTY, not "port= followed by a guarded number". The prose word "guard" and "a hole after an atom" (draft:1944) describe an atom-level binding the precedence does not give; the user must write `(?:(\d+)&${<256})` to guard one atom. A silent empty program is the G0 failure class (the compiler's empty-intersection error at a narrowing site may catch the second form; the first is a rx-text `&`, not a stated narrowing site, §5.2 draft:1394).
- Fix shape: state the scope in §11 (a guard covers the whole concatenation of its alternative; group it to guard one atom) and add the look-alike row (`/(\d+)&${<256}/` vs `/x(\d+)&${<256}/`).

### CENSUS-12 (CONFIRMED; severity design (small); effort S; blast radius §4.3 duplicate-label sentence, L64 (rev 10), §13 O35): rev 10's L64 example is a label both DUPLICATE and REPEATED, where §4.3 gives `$x` two rules
