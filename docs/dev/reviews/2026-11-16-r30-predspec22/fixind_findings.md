### FIXIND-1  (CONFIRMED)  severity: design
- where: rev 22 §8 `when(c, p)` (copy l.3338-3342), changelog rev 22 (A) "`when`'s body (`(x & !q)`...)", §4.2 rev-22 table row "§8 `when(c, p)`" (l.2164); against §5.2 RULE "THE OPERAND IS NEVER AN ARGUMENT", "No lambda", §5.1 "ONE kind of binding" (a body is one expression), §2.1 ONE INPUT RULE, §2.2 WHAT `!` TAKES.
- claim: the fix ADDS a body that is not writable in the language. Rev 20-21's `when` was the pointwise macro `(c & p) | !c` with its operand open (the `&` fills it; §2.1 typing: "operators on macros are POINTWISE"), so `x & when(c,p)` = `(x & c & p) | (x & !c)` was a consequence, not the body. Rev 22's `(x & c & p) | (x & !q)` with `q := x & c` needs the body to NAME THE OPERAND x (to build q) and to hold a LOCAL BINDING. Neither exists: the operand is never an argument and reaches a body only through `&` (§5.2, §2.3), a body is one expression (§5.1), no lambda (§5.2). The operand-open spelling is now illegal: the only operand-open form of "x & c" is the section `(& c)`, which §2.1 says IS c, so `!(& c)` = `!c` = the new §4.1 ERROR. So `when` cannot be a library macro of the language any more; it is either a special item (a built-in that can see its operand) or a new mechanism (a body that names its operand). Same for ANY author macro that wants "not c" for an operand it is given: e.g. `nonblank_unless(c) := ...` — the error's two rewrites both fail inside a body (the set complement changes the question, §2.2 L30; the named pattern needs the operand's name). The 0-of-1,110 check (PART A (A4)) computes the new body from outside (`left`, `qn` built over x by the script), so it shows the ANSWERS agree, not that the body is expressible.
- repro (text): try to write `when(c, p) := ...` in rev 22: `(c & p) | !c` -> ERROR (`!` on a condition, §4.1 last bullet); `(c & p) | !(& c)` -> `(& c)` = c (§2.1), same ERROR; `(c & p) | !(x & c)` -> x unbound (operand never an argument, §5.2); `q := ...` inside a body -> no local bindings (§5.1).
- effort: small to state (pick one: (a) `when` becomes a built-in, with the special item counted in G0b's kind count; or (b) the general form in STANDING QUESTIONS (2) below: `!` on a macro with its operand open means "the operand minus (operand & M)", DOMs read in (operand & M), which is exactly what the error's second rewrite already computes, and gives `when` back as `(c & p) | !c`). Blast radius: §8 `when`, §2.1 typing sentence, the kind table's `!B` row, every author macro that negates a condition passed to it (the old `!c` in a body is now an error with no in-body fix).

## STANDING QUESTIONS

(1) SIBLING OF A FAMILY. Two families, both with the rev 22 addition as their third (or later) member:
- THE NEW ERROR is the UNAPPLIED-MACRO ERROR (§2.3) at one more site. §2.3 already reads "an unapplied macro where an rx is required is a compile error naming the fix", with members (i) a macro in a hole `${(x: digit)}`, (ii) a macro beside an rx under `|` (`C | !B`, §2.1 "rx | macro mixes kinds"), and the owner's ruling makes `!`'s operand (iii) one more "rx required" position ("`!` takes an rx"). Stated as its own §4.1 bullet with its own text, it is a parallel mechanism; stated as "`!`'s operand is an rx position" it needs no new rule, only a per-site hint (FIXIND-3's hint table). Lean: fold it into §2.3, keep the hints. This also settles FIXIND-8's tension in one place.
- THE COMPLEMENTED PATTERN'S OWN DOM SCOPE is the third member of "a sub-expression reduced to its STRING SET before use": (i) a SET POSITION (an assertion's argument, §5.6: "decided on strings"), (ii) a labelled pattern on the VALUE side of `:` / in a hole, (iii) now `!q`. For (i) the draft says how labels inside read (they do not reach the outside); for (ii) I found no DOM-scope sentence; (iii) has the manager's readings (1)-(3). One rule covers all three: "a pattern reduced to a set is a complete program; its labels' DOMs are its own and never reach out". Lean: state it once (§4.2 THE DOMAIN), point the three sites at it; the trigger is any site where a labelled pattern stands inside another program's set position (E-CAL-style value-side patterns already do).
(2) GENERAL FORM + UNLOCKS. The general form of not-is-complement: `!` is the complement RELATIVE TO A CONTEXT PROGRAM, `C & !X` = "C's matches minus (C & X)'s", with X's DOMs read in (C & X). The ruled case is C = the whole program written by hand (`P & !(P & c)`); the old `!c` read C's DOMs (rev 21's N1), which is what the ruling removed. What it unlocks: a RELATIVE COMPLEMENT ON A MACRO WITH ITS OPERAND OPEN, `x & !c` := `x & !(x & c)` evaluated once the operand is filled, i.e. the error's second rewrite done by the compiler at the `&`, with the complemented pattern's own-DOM readings. That gives back `when(c, p) := (c & p) | !c` as a plain library macro (FIXIND-1), every author macro that negates a passed condition, and `!some(c)` = `occurs.none(c)` as a consequence rather than a special hint. It does NOT reintroduce the 17:29 problem (an added condition outside q never narrows q's DOMs; FIXIND-2's caveat applies to both). TRIGGER: the first library or §12 macro that must negate a condition it is given (today: `when`), or an owner request for `!` in macro bodies. It is a design change against his literal words ("That has no meaning on a condition"), so it goes to him as a question (slug: relative-complement), not into the draft as a reading.
