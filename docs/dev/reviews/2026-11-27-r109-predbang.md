## ANSWERS TO THE OWNER'S QUESTIONS

(Runs by the examples and general lenses with their own models, python `re`; PCRE2 could differ only on

**1. "what is the difference between (a:! 5) and !(a: 5) ? perhaps its in the compound forms."**
They differ exactly where the text on the left can be ABSENT: `(a :! 5)` needs `a` present and not 5;
`!(a: 5)` also holds when `a` is absent. Crosswise, `(a :! 5)` = `!(a ?: 5)` and `!(a: 5)` = `(a ?:! 5)`.
- Plain label, on `/(?<h>\d)?x/`: `(h :! <5)` WHOLE `x` FAILS, `!(h: <5)` WHOLE `x` HOLDS; SEARCHED on `7x x`
  both give `7x`@0; FIND-ALL on `7x 3x x`: [`7x`] against [`7x`, `x`@4, `x`@6].
- The compound forms are where it shows most, as he guessed:
  - A record: `(a :! S, b :! T)` means "neither", `!(a: S, b: T)` means "not both": 71 of 121 label states
    differ, 50 of them with both labels present (F4). The entry-wise form of `!(a: S, b: T)` is
    `(a ?:! S) | (b ?:! T)` (0 of 121). On `/(?<a>\d)?,(?<b>\d)?/` with S `<5`, T `>=3`: WHOLE `3,1`: FAILS
    against HOLDS; SEARCHED `3,4 7,1`: `7,1`@4 against `3,`@0; FIND-ALL `3,4 7,1 ,4 6,`: [`7,1`] against
    [`3,`, `7,1`, `,4`, `6,`].
  - A joint entry `((a, b) :! >= (5, 3))` against `!((a, b): >= (5, 3))`: 21 of 121 differ (one part absent).
  - A loop: on `/(?:(?<d>\d)?,)+/`, the text `7,,` leaves d unset (the last pass's own value), so `(d :! "7")`
    FAILS and `!(d: "7")` HOLDS, though PCRE's capture would still say `7`; SEARCHED `x7,, 1,`: `1,`@5 against
    `7,,`@1; FIND-ALL `7,, 1,7, ,`: [`1,`@4] against [`7,,`, `1,`, `,`, `,`].
  - In an `occurrences` argument the hit list moves: `occurrences(/(?<h>\d)?x/ & ...)` on `x 3x 7x` hits [`7x`]
    with `(h :! <5)`, [`x`, `x`, `7x`] with `!(h: <5)`.
  - Inside a group question (`some`, `occurs.none`) there is NO difference: a setting is never absent (0 of 1093).

**2. "if !(cond) is ok, is there another place ! would also work?"**
Yes, wherever the operand is itself a zero-width test, `!` keeps the one meaning "this cannot hold here":
- On `^A` and `~A`: `!^A` is `(?!A)` and `!~A` is `(?<!A)` (0 of 364 against PCRE's own forms). Then `^!` and
  `~!` are not needed as operators at all. This is the variant marked V-C, and the suggestion below.
- As the test of `when`, for the else-only form: `when(!(h: <5), z)`, `when(!^x, z)`.
- On a group question: `!some(c)` (today's `NOT some(c)`, the same question as `occurs.none(c)`).
- `!!X` works by composition and has X's answers, but passes no labels on (0 of 121 against `^A`; labels differ):
  legal, with a warning that `^X` keeps the labels.
- On a consuming pattern, `!A` = PCRE's `(?!A)` (V-D): it works and keeps the same meaning, "A cannot match from
  here". On a zero-width operand that is just the logical not. This is a relaxation the owner can take later with
  no answer moving (see the suggestion).
- It does NOT work as `|!` (an `|` asks no yes/no question, so there is nothing to negate), or on a `records`
  item. `A & !B` as the old difference spelling is gone in every variant: the compile error names `&!`.

**3. "would it make sense to replace :! et al if we went with !(cond) or do they mean different things"**
In part:
- `?:!` adds nothing. `(a ?:! S)` is exactly `!(a: S)` in every mode and place where `?:!` is legal, and it is
  refused in more places (a value left of `:`, an index, a joint left part, a group question). Where it is
  refused, `!(..)` is the only form that keeps the printed answers. Replace it: drop `?:!`, which also removes the
  form he called a "monstrosity".
- `:!` means something different: "present and outside". With `!` alone that is the double negative
  `!(a ?: S)`, or `(a: anything &! S)` (0 of 18 cells). It is the direct spelling of today's printed `(h: !<5)`.
  Keep it, but only on a single entry for now: inside a record it is a De Morgan trap (F4).
- `&!` keeps a meaning `!(cond)` cannot give alone. It is the difference of stretches, `A &! B` =
  `A & !(0: B)`, but only beside `&`: in a sequence, `0` is the open prefix, not A's match. With the complement
  object gone it is the only short form of "A but not a B". Keep it, at `&`'s precedence, read left to right
  (F6).

## SUGGESTION ON `!`

**Recommended: V-C, with the prefix `!` stated as V-D's meaning, and two of the three marks kept.**

1. `!X` means "X cannot hold here": it consumes nothing and passes no labels on. For now X must be a zero-width
   test: a condition, `^A`, `~A`, a group question, or another `!X`. A consuming X (`!A` on a plain pattern) is a
   compile error that names `!^A` (or `&!` when the `!` stands as an `&` operand, `:!` / `&!` inside a set when it
   stands after a label: F5, F8).
2. The lookarounds are `^A` (A matches ahead; it replaces `peek(A)` and passes A's labels on) and `~A` (A matches
   behind). Their negatives are `!^A` and `!~A`. `^!` and `~!` are not operators (F9).
3. `when(T, B, C) := T SEQ B | !^T SEQ C`: one body for every kind of test (F3). A bare test consumes as today.
   `^` on a zero-width test is that test (lib/control/peek's reading), so the guard on a condition is just
   `!(c)`, and the owner's "abuse" form `^!(cond)` never appears.
4. Entries: `:` `?:` `:!`. `?:!` is a reserved error naming `!(a: S)`. `:!` inside a record is an error for now
   (F4).
5. `A &! B` is the difference, at `&`'s level, read left to right. A condition on the right of `&!` is an error
   naming `A & !(c)` (F1).
6. The complement object leaves; a bare `!B` with B consuming is the error in 1. match-and-filter is restated or
   retired as its own question (F10).

Why this variant:
- It meets Goal 1 with one sentence for `!` that never mentions its operand's kind. The restriction to
  zero-width tests is a stated rule, as Goal 1 allows. The owner's "!(cond) and !A mean different things" was
  true of the complement. Here the complement's meaning lives only in `:!` and `&!`, so `!` has one meaning.
- It gives the one `when` body that V-B cannot (F3).
- It folds every printed `NOT` to `!` mechanically, with every printed answer kept. V-A leaves three ruled tests
  with no spelling (F2).
- It sits on the conservative chain: V-D is V-C with one error relaxed, and no answer moves
  (core/principles/conservative-first).

What it reverses in the record:
- What every variant reverses:
  - the complement object: not-is-complement's first half, complement-place, lone-complement-match's object;
  - drop-not item 2's spelling, `peek(!A)` -> `!^A`;
  - match-and-filter's only named filter-only member.
- Beyond those, drop-not item 3's first error: `!peek(A)`, as `!^A`, becomes legal. That turns an error into an
  answer, which conservative-first allows, but it is a ruled error.

What it keeps:
- condition-not and absent-under-not;
- "Use NOT to get else behavior";
- the `when(!x, z)` error for a bare x (its message now names `!^x`);
- drop-not readings (c) and (d), and capture-negated (its scope re-spelled, F7).

Line 6 of the candidate leaves.

What it costs:
- A named complement (`not_seven := !seven`) is gone; the user names the positive and writes `&!`.
- PCRE's `(?!A)` is spelled `!^A`, one character more than `!A`. This is the owner's Goal 2 concern, and the
  trigger for relaxing to V-D. He can take V-D now instead. V-D costs F8's two sentences and F9's token rule, and
  reverses drop-not item 3 entire plus lone-complement-match's error (record 11757-11758; the round's
  examples and general lenses found no answer-level obstacle to it).
- The fold:
  - re-spell 13 `NOT peek` spans and 34 condition-not spans;
  - write the precedence rows (F6), the effect scope (F7) and the family table with its DFA column (F11, F13).

Lens leanings, for the record (from each lens's own question): ruling: V-B (fewest reversals); examples: V-B
