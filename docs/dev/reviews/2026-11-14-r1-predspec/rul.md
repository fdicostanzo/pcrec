- Record: DEC:10170, D193 add. 3 (Frank, 2026-11-10; D193 add. 4 DEC:10172 says D193 rules MEANINGS): in a predicate's regex test on a label, "inside the slashes `^` and `$` are the LABEL's start and end (the label is the whole subject there)", so `a =~ /^Y$/` is the whole-label test; scan_substring's in-context anchoring "does not carry over to our own syntax".
- Subject: §11 (S:661-665) context-marked sets, `^` constrains the LEFT CONTEXT and a label condition's value carries its context; §13 C27 (S:928) and O17 (S:953) cite rev 0 and D196 F7 only; D193 add. 3 is cited nowhere (grep `D193` in the subject: only §0/§15 background). Q1's ruling (CF:586) says SUPERSEDES "value-scoped literals" (the CF:56 note) but names no D-row.
- Witness (kit): `python3 -I q.py and '(?<k>\w+)(?==)' '^S\w*$' 'a Sx=1'` -> None (in-context, Model A); `--isolated` -> (2,4) 'Sx' (D193 add. 3 reading). So `k: /^S\w*$/` or `$k ~ /^S/` answers differently under the two rulings; D193 add. 3 also states the search form (`=~`, now `~`), which §2.6 (S:175) leaves silent on `^`.
- Recommend: §13 lists D193 add. 3 as a ruled reading that Q1 (TENTATIVE) would supersede, and the critic round's Q1 comparison counts it as Model B's ruled precedent; under Model A the spec must say whether `$x ~ /^Y/` (search in a VALUE) still binds `^` to the value's start (it is a value, not a label-in-context: Model A's own §4.1 says `$x` is a one-string set).
- Effort small (doc); blast radius every anchored predicate literal; §11, §2.6, §13.

### RUL-2 (CONFIRMED, answer): "`!<200` and `>=200` agree on every target" is false; the record's claim is wrong too
- Record: CF:382 ("`!<200` is a filter, `>=200` a full rx; equal on any target (positive form is a lint hint)"). Subject §2.2 (S:118): "they agree on every target". C9 ruling (CF:571, OWNER RULED): comparisons are on the STRING SET, integer sections hold canonical numerals only (S:147-149).
- Witness (rul/parse.py): target "abc": `"abc" & !<200` = {"abc"}, `"abc" & >=200` = empty; target "007": `!<200` admits, `>=200` rejects (non-canonical); "250": both admit. `/\w+/ & !<200` matches `abc`, `/\w+/ & >=200` does not.
- Consequence: the "positive form is a lint hint" rewrite would CHANGE ANSWERS; and the two forms differ in kind as well (`${>=200}` and `C | >=200` legal, `${!<200}`/`C | !<200` the unapplied-macro error, S:117), a look-alike pair behaving differently on both axes (owner criterion 1).
- Recommend: state `!<200` = complement relative to the TARGET (all non-numerals and padded numerals pass) and drop the equivalence and the lint; or define a lint only under a numeral-typed target (`/\d+/ & ...` canonical part). Effort small; blast radius every negated comparison; §2.2.

### RUL-3 (CONFIRMED by the spec text, answer/design): §4.5's "merged narrowing" contradicts extensional `&` + some-parse
- Record: Q2 (CF:586, OWNER RULED) some parse passes; `&` extensional (CF:329, AGREED; owner criterion 3); CF:48-49 ("every occurrence of the same application denotes ONE parse"; the `a<5 & b<5` on "123" example: TRUE per-test, FALSE merged).
- Subject §4.5 (S:351-354) states both: a condition passes if SOME parse passes, AND "every occurrence of the same application denotes ONE parse (merged narrowing)". §4.1: `x: S` is a SET of whole matches, combined by extensional `&`.
- Witness (rul/parse.py): `/(?<a>\d+)(?<b>\d+)/ & (a: <5) & (b: <5)` on whole subject "123": set(a:<5) holds via parse (1,23), set(b:<5) via (12,3), so extensional `&` = TRUE; merged one-parse = FALSE (neither parse has both). PCRE's chosen parse (12,3) = FALSE.
- Consequence: the only reading consistent with criterion 3 is TRUE, but then `(a: <5) & (b: <5)` and a hypothetical single-parse record differ, and the default level (TRUE) disagrees with `vm` (FALSE) on the same program beyond what the `label.ambiguous` warning describes (it is per LABEL, this is per PAIR of labels: both a and b are ambiguous here, so the warning fires, but its "consequence" text must say multi-label conditions may be satisfied by DIFFERENT parses).
- Not in §13 (C11 resolved only the single-label case). Recommend a C-row: Reading A per-condition some-parse (extensional, TRUE); Reading B one parse per program (merged, FALSE, needs a product over parses, not extensional). Effort small (doc), design decision for the owner; §4.5, §4.1.

### RUL-4 (CONFIRMED by the spec text, design): right-associative `&` breaks the AGREED chain label-lookup rule
- Record: CF:274 (01:00, AGREED per S:299): "in a chain `P & Q & x: …` the label is looked up in `P & Q`; a label present in both is AMBIGUOUS ... fix: rename, or scope `(P & x: …) & Q`" (written for left grouping). Right associativity is a MANAGER LEAN (CF:633, S:114).
- Subject §2.2 (S:114) `a & b & c` = `a & (b & c)`; §4.1 (S:291-292) keeps the 01:00 lookup rule verbatim. Under right association `P & Q & (x: S)` = `P & (Q & (x: S))`: the inner `Q & (x: S)` is an rx applied to a condition, so x must be a label of Q alone (§4.1: unknown label = error), and a label in BOTH P and Q is never ambiguous (Q's wins silently).
- Witness: `/(?<x>\d+),\d+/ & /.*,5/ & (x: <200)`: lookup rule = valid (x from P); right-assoc = "no label x in /.*,5/" error. Swap P and Q and it compiles: operand order now decides validity, against "`&` commutes, order is readability only" (CF:374) and criterion 3.
- Not in §13 (C22 is the `name:` pipeline only). Recommend: either conditions resolve against the whole `&`-chain (flattened, n-ary `&`, transforms then need their own operator, see RUL-11) or the lookup rule is restated for right association. Effort small; blast radius every multi-operand condition chain; §2.2, §4.1.

### RUL-5 (CONFIRMED by the spec text, design): §2.6's `$x !~ rx` desugars to a form §4.1 makes an error
- Subject §2.6 (S:177): "`$x !~ rx` is a TEST: `$x & !contains(rx)`". §4.1 (S:288-289, AGREED 01:00, CF:273): "`$x & ...` used AS A CONDITION is an ERROR: did you mean `x: ...`?".
- So the spec's own definition of `!~` is the trap §4.1 forbids; the consistent spelling is `x: !contains(rx)` (and `$x ~ rx` as a test would be `x: contains(rx)`). Adds a consequence to O12: once `!` is a filter and `x:` is the one condition form (Q6 concept, OWNER RULED), `!~` is a second spelling of `x: !contains(...)` and the search form `~` remains only as the VALUE (occurrence-set) producer.
- Recommend: define `!~` as sugar for `x: !contains(rx)` or drop it (O12 Reading B), and restate §2.6 with `x:`. Effort trivial; §2.6, §4.1, §13 O12.

### RUL-6 (CONFIRMED by the spec text, design): every passing `@assert.none` is an empty-intersection ERROR
- Record: CF:144 (2026-11-13 evening, LEAN, carried in §5.2 S:420-421): "ERROR when the empty set comes only from what the author wrote, pure parts (types, literal arguments, library parts) ... it is always a bug". Q9 concept OWNER RULED (CF:597), assertion library form Frank (CF:608).

### RUL-8 (CONFIRMED by the spec text, design; criterion 1): `isodate(x).year` (a value) vs `(x & isodate).year` (a pattern), declared equal
- Subject §2.3 (S:137-138): `p(x)` = `x & p` for a parameterless filter. §5.2 (S:413-414): FORWARD `isodate(x)`: text -> fields (CF:48 "isodate(x).year = projection of the application", a VALUE). §3.2 tool 4 (S:242) and CF:72: `.name` on an rx is MAP-VIEW navigation, structural, yielding the part's PATTERN (`isodate.year = \d{4}`).
- So `isodate(x).year` is a value by §5.2 and the pattern `(x & isodate).year` by §2.3 + §3.2 (and C17 leaves the structure of `x & isodate` unstated). The cut layer's note (CF:516, §9 cut) had resolved it the other way: "`(X & isodate).weekday` = a value".
- Recommend: state which: (A) application-dot is a VALUE only on `$`-forms (`$d.year`, already the value spelling, §4.1) and `.` on any rx is structural; drop "forward `isodate(x)`" as a value form, or (B) `p(x)` is not `x & p` for labelled patterns. (A) fits "`$x` ALWAYS a value" (CF:273). Not in §13 (C20 covers transforms only). Effort small; §2.3, §3.2, §5.2.

### RUL-9 (CONFIRMED by the spec text, answer): `X[<4]` contains index 0, which is "the whole rx"
- Subject §3.2 (S:234-237): `[]` takes "integers, integer sets, numeric filters (`X[1]`, `X[<4]`)", "`[0]` = the whole rx (PCRE group 0)", 1-based (OWNER RULED, Q7). §2.4: `<4` = canonical integers {0,1,2,3}.
- Witness: `/a|b|c|d/[<4]` = either `[a, b, c]` (if 0 is excluded from filters) or `[(a|b|c|d), a, b, c]` (0 included as an entry), whose language is the whole rx: `X[<4]` then equals `X` as a language while `X[between(1,3)]` does not. Look-alike `X[<4]` vs `X[1|2|3]` (criterion 1).

### RUL-11 (CONFIRMED by the spec text, design; slot tracing): `&` serves two mechanisms (intersection and pullback); transforms have an unstated second application form
- Subject §2.7 (S:191-202, MANAGER LEAN CF:633): a transform on the LEFT of `&` is the pullback of the rest of its chain; parentheses end its scope; a trailing transform is a no-op warning. §2.2: `&` extensional, associative "for pure rx". So `x & unpad & between(1, 12)` != `(x & unpad) & between(1, 12)` (the second = no-op warning + canonical filter = {10, 11, 12} on `/\d\d/`), and the reader cannot tell from `x & t & y` whether `t` is an rx (commutes) or a transform (scopes `y`) without looking up `t`'s kind: a look-alike pair (criterion 1).
- Second form: §4.4 and §12 apply value mappings by CALL on values: `days($b)`, `weekday_of($year, ...)`, `days_in($month, leap($year))` (S:332-334, 754, 784), while §2.3 (S:138) says "Transforms are applied only as pipeline stages" and S:209 supersedes `unpad(S)`. FORWARD image on a value (`t($x)`) and PULLBACK on a set (`t & S`) are both used, only the second is stated.
- Unstated law the spec should carry (two spellings, one meaning): for a single-label computed label, `m := t($x); P & (m: S)` == `P & (x: t & S)`; and `month := $mname.index` (§12.6) is a third spelling of the month-name -> number transform §2.7 lists. Recommend: a distinct pipeline operator (placeholder `|>`, not alphabetic) for pullbacks, leaving `&` purely extensional; state forward application `t($x)` for values. Effort medium (doc); blast radius §2.3, §2.7, §4.4, C20, C22 (both dissolve with a separate operator). Family RUL-F1.

### RUL-15 (consequence for C16, answer): the misread precedence widens silently instead of failing
- D196 (DEC:10195, RULED): `&` binds tighter than `|`. CF:251's example `x & <256 | >300` therefore parses `(x & <256) | >300`; `>300` is a label-free filter, hence a FULL rx (§2.1 S:98-99), so the program also matches ANY canonical numeral above 300 anywhere, with no unapplied-macro error to catch it (a label-carrying filter would have errored). Recommend: keep D196, withdraw the CF:251 example, and warn when a label-free comparison stands as a top-level `|` operand beside an `&`-applied one (a named warning, e.g. `precedence.section`). §2.2, §2.4.

### RUL-16 (SUSPECTED, design): two rules for a label that holds more than one occurrence

## STANDING QUESTIONS

### SIBLING OF A FAMILY (near-duplicate mechanisms; slot tracing part (b))
- **RUL-F1. Value mappings (4 members):** transforms/pullbacks (§2.7), computed-label bindings (§4.4), `.index` accept-class values (§3.1, §6.2), forward value calls `days($b)`/`weekday_of(...)` (§4.4, §12). All are ONE object: a finite transducer (or relation, when it reads several labels) from labelled text to a value. Unify as "a mapping has a forward image on values (`t($x)`) and a pullback on sets (`x: t |> S`)", with `.index` the built-in mapping of an array entry and a computed label = a named multi-input mapping. Deletes C20, C22, RUL-11's look-alike, and the month-name triple spelling. Trigger: now (design text only); the build needs one transducer construction either way.
- **RUL-F2. Quantifiers and emptiness checks (6+ members):** `some/all` over sets (§2.6), `occurs.all/some/none/exactly/at_least/at_most` (§8), the sequence rule (§4.3), `count` (C21), `@assert.some/none/same` (§5.6), and the IMPLICIT checks: empty-intersection ERROR and `dead.branch` (§5.2), typed-parameter containment, `empty.search` (§7), overlapping map keys/overloads (CF:242, CF:168). General form: `Q(source, cond)` with Q in {none, some, all, exactly n, at_least n, at_most n} and source in {occurrences of a search, iterations of a repeated label, entries of an array, strings of a pattern (compile time), parses}; `some`=at_least 1, `none`=exactly 0, `all`=none(!cond); a compile-time assertion is `Q` over "strings of a pattern" made an `@` effect; each implicit diagnostic is a NAMED implicit assertion with a default severity, controlled by `@warn.*`. Unlocks: RUL-6's polarity clash disappears (an explicit `@assert.none(X)` supersedes the implicit check on X); `@warn.error` covers every implicit check uniformly; one cost rule (capped counter n+2) for every source. Trigger: when the second of `occurs` / search quantifiers reaches a build, or now for the spec text.
- **RUL-F3. Same-text-twice (4 members):** `\k<x>` (vm, strict subset), `${$x}` value holes (priced by leftovers), `enumerate` + `each` (E-SEP), [BACKREF-FINITE] (engine row, Q10). Trigger for the engine half: Q10's own (its census); the spec half (equal levels for look-alike spellings) is criterion 1, now. RUL-7.
- **RUL-F4. Fold `where_group` by name into the same "set of names as an rx" rule (`A.select(startsWith("S"))` or `where_group(startsWith("S"), A)`); `X.name` is then the one-name case. Trigger: now (doc).
- **RUL-F5. The dot (3 resolution rules):** navigation, module members, element metadata (RUL-12). Modules-as-maps already made members navigation; moving metadata to call-position built-ins makes `.` one rule.
- **RUL-F6. Application (4 spellings):** `p(x)` = `x & p`, positional construction `between(lo, hi)`, keyword filtering `isodate(year: ...)`, forward value call `days($b)`. RUL-8 and RUL-10 are where they disagree; general form: `f(args)` constructs/configures, `&` (or `:`) filters, `$`-forms are values; `p(x)` = `x & p` then holds only for filters, never for labelled patterns used as values.

### GENERAL FORM + UNLOCKS (per rule)
- **§2.2 prefix `!` (filter):** general form = complement RELATIVE TO THE TARGET's declared universe. Unlock: typed sections (`!<200` over a numeral-typed target = `>=200`), making the lint of CF:382 correct where it applies (RUL-2). Trigger: a library type (int, decimal) that declares its universe.
- **§4.5 some-parse:** general form = a parse quantifier (`some`/`all`/PCRE-chosen over parses, CF:75 (3)), i.e. RUL-F2 with source = parses.
