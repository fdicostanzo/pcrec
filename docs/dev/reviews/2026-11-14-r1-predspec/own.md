# OWN findings: crit-predspec

## Findings

### OWN-1 CONFIRMED (answer, design; effort S; blast: every `&` chain holding a transform) Reordering or parenthesizing an `&` chain silently changes its answer once a transform is in it (§2.2, §2.7, §8)
Rule: §2.7 "a TRANSFORM ON THE LEFT OF `&` is the pullback of the rest of its chain ... parentheses end its scope"; §2.2 "no change for pure rx"; owner note (C1) "`&` commutes, so order is readability only".
Witnesses (all by the draft's own definitions):
- (a) the draft's own E-FILE line `alts & !startsWith("#") & length & <=20` reordered to `alts & length & <=20 & !startsWith("#")`: the `#` test now runs on the LENGTH NUMERAL (never starts with `#`), so it is vacuous; `# words` (7 chars) is admitted. No `transform.noop` (the transform is not trailing), no `dead.branch` (nothing is empty). SILENT.
- (b) `/\d\d/ & unpad & between(1, 12)` vs `(/\d\d/ & unpad) & between(1, 12)`: on `05` the first matches, the second is `/\d\d/` (trailing-transform no-op, WARN) then `{10,11,12}`: no match. Parentheses that are pure grouping for every other `&` operand change the answer.
- (c) binding vs its expansion: `m := /\d\d/ & unpad` then `m & between(1, 12)` = the (b) second reading ({10,11,12}); textually inlining `m` gives the first. A binding is not its expansion.
- (d) `x & foo & bar`: whether `bar` filters `x` or `foo(x)`'s image depends on whether the imported name `foo` is a transform or a filter, which the text does not show (E-JSONL: `json.jstr & dates.isodate & ...`; if a library later turns a filter into a transform, every caller chain changes meaning).
Criterion 1 (look-alike) and criterion 3 in spirit: `a & b & c` "is intersection" except when one operand is a transform. Recommendation: give the pullback its OWN non-alphabetic stage mark (spelling open; not `&`), so `&` stays pure, commutative, associative intersection with no positional meaning; or, minimally, an ERROR (not a warning) for any transform not followed by a filter inside the same parenthesized chain, plus a lint for a raw-text test placed after a transform. Adds consequence to §13 C1/C20/C22.

### OWN-2 CONFIRMED (answer, doc; effort S; blast: every negated comparison) `!<200` and `>=200` do NOT agree on every target, and only one of them may stand alone (§2.2, §2.4, §5.2, §7.1)
Rule: §2.2 "`!<200` is a filter; `>=200` is a full rx; they agree on every target." §2.4: an integer section contains CANONICAL `\d+` numerals only.
Witnesses:
- `/\d{4}/ & !<200` contains `0150` (not a canonical numeral, so not in `<200`, so kept by the complement); `/\d{4}/ & >=200` does not (`0150` is not canonical, so not in `>=200`). Same for `"abc" & !<200` = {"abc"} vs `"abc" & >=200` = empty. The draft's sentence is false whenever the target holds non-canonical text, which is exactly the padded-field case §2.4 warns about: `/\d\d/ & !between(1, 12)` ("invalid months") keeps `05`.
- Standalone use: `${>=200}` and `@warn.off(startsWith("dead."))` are legal (label-free filter = full rx, §2.1, §7.1) but `${!<200}` and `@warn.off(!startsWith("dead."))` are the unapplied-macro error (§2.2: `!` is ALWAYS a filter). Two forms the draft calls equivalent differ in where they may be written; a label-free `!f` is a well-defined (regular) set yet is refused, and §2.1 ("a value filter mentioning NO label ... is a full rx") and §2.2 ("prefix `!` is ALWAYS a filter") contradict each other on `!<200` itself (not in §13).
Recommendation: (1) correct §2.2 to "agree on canonical-numeral targets; `!<200` also admits every non-numeral" and add a `compare.width`-style lint when a negated numeric section meets a target with non-canonical members; (2) make label-free `!f` a full rx exactly as label-free `f` is (complement relative to all strings is regular; the "useless in a search" argument is about the search hazard, which §7's nullable/everywhere rule already catches), so the look-alike pair behaves alike.

### OWN-3 CONFIRMED (answer, design; effort S-M; blast: every comparison against a binding or a computed value) Two bindings that are the SAME set compare differently, so comparisons are not extensional (§2.1, §2.4, §3.4, §5.6)
Rule: §2.1 "`5` = {\"5\"} ... they differ only in what a comparison means"; §2.4 "The LITERAL decides the reading"; §3.4 lists comparisons as EXTENSIONAL ("depend only on the language").
Witness:

    a := 256
    b := "256"
    @assert.same(a, b)        # passes: both are {"256"}
    x := /\d+/
    x & <a                    # integers 0..255: "1000" out
    x & <b                    # lexicographic: "1000" in ("1000" < "256")

Either the reading travels with the binding (then two extensionally equal values behave differently under an operation §3.4 calls extensional: criterion 3 broken, and `@assert.same` certifies a false equivalence) or it does not (then `<a` has no reading and §4.6's typed value-vs-value rule applies to a constant, contradicting §2.4). The draft states neither; the same question hits arithmetic (`$y + 1`: §2.5 gives `+` but §2.1 says "no number type", and on a captured `0099` nothing decides whether `${$y + 1}` inserts `100` or `0100`; `/(?<y>\d{4})-${$y + 1}/` on `0099-0100` vs `0099-100`), and leading-zero literals (§2.4 "no leading zeros ... unless the literal has them": does `between(01, 12)` admit `1`? `05`? `10`?; `between(1, 12)` vs `between(01, 12)` look alike and the rule does not say).
Recommendation: make the READING a visible property of the comparison, not of the literal's quoting: e.g. numeric vs lexicographic as two section spellings (the literal then only supplies the bound), with a binding of a bare numeral usable only as a bound. Then `<a` = `<b` (same bound, same section), and quoting stops being semantic. State the leading-zero and arithmetic-result widths explicitly (lean: arithmetic yields canonical numerals; width is a `pad(n)` stage).

### OWN-4 CONFIRMED (answer, design; effort S; blast: every negated condition, every optional or repeated label) `!(x: S)`, `x: !S`, `occurs.none(x: S)` and `occurs.all(x: S)` look interchangeable and split three ways by label shape (§4.2, §4.3, §8; extends C28)
Rules: §4.2 "a comparison on an UNSET label is false (its `!` true); a condition naming a label that is unset for a match fails"; §4.3 sequence rule: `x: S` constrains ALL iterations.
Witnesses:
- Repeated: `p := /(?:(?<n>\d),)+/` on `1,7,`: `p & !(n: <5)` MATCHES (not all below 5); `p & (n: !<5)` does NOT (1 is below 5); `p & occurs.none(n: <5)` does not. For a single label the first two coincide; a user cannot tell from the condition which reading applies, only from the pattern's shape (a `+` far away).
- Unset: `q := /(?<x>\d)?z/` on `z`: `q & !(x: <5)` = true (the negation of a failing condition); `q & (x: !<5)`: §4.2's first sentence says the `!` of a comparison on an unset label is TRUE, its second says a condition naming an unset label FAILS. Underdetermined (two readings, both plausible).
- Zero iterations: `r := /(?:(?<n>\d),)*end/` on `end`: `r & (n: <5)` is TRUE under the sequence rule (all of zero iterations) but FALSE under §4.2 if a never-entered repeated label is "unset"; meanwhile `/(?:(?<n>\d),)?end/ & (n: <5)` on `end` is FALSE (unset). `?` vs `*` around the same group flips the answer on the same subject.
- C28 consequence: if `x: S` = `occurs.all(x: S)`, the library form is a synonym (harmless but two spellings); if not, `x: S` means something unstated on repeated labels.
Recommendation: one rule for all label shapes: a condition `x: S` holds iff EVERY occurrence of x is in S AND x occurs at least once (so zero iterations = unset = fail, `?` and `*` agree); `!(x: S)` is its plain complement; `x: !S` = every occurrence outside S. Then say in the spec, as a look-alike table row, that `!(x: S)` and `x: !S` differ (and the compiler warns `label.unset`/a new `negation.scope` when they differ for the pattern at hand, showing a witness, in the ambiguity-warning style). Make `occurs.all` an alias or remove it.

### OWN-5 CONFIRMED (answer, doc; effort S; blast: every filter applied to a multi-alternative rx) The three "equivalent" application spellings disagree on an alternative that lacks the label (§2.3 vs §4.2, §3.2, §3.4)
Rule: §2.3 "Application has three equivalent spellings for FILTERS: `x & (a: digit)`, `(a: digit)(x)`, `each((a: digit), A)` (an alternative lacking label `a` is an error naming it)". §4.2: "`(x = /\d/ | y = /[a-z]/) & (x: /[0-5]/)` = `[0-5]`" (the alternative lacking x is silently DROPPED, the D196-consistent reading).
Witness: `A := (x = /\d/ | y = /[a-z]/)`:
- `A & (x: /[0-5]/)` = `[0-5]` (§4.2);
- `(x: /[0-5]/)(A)` = the same (by `p(x)` = `x & p`);
- `each((x: /[0-5]/), A)` = ERROR "alternative 2 lacks label x" (§2.3's parenthetical) — or, if the parenthetical covers all three spellings, `A & (x: ...)` is an error and §4.2's own example is wrong.
Second divergence even when every entry has the label: `each` is STRUCTURAL (§5.3: keeps labels and order, returns an array), `&` distributes with unstated entry structure (C17), so `(A & f)[2]`, `.index` and `enumerate`-like reads can differ from `each(f, A)[2]`.
Recommendation: drop `each` from the equivalence list (it is the structural per-entry map; say so), keep `x & f` = `f(x)` as the one extensional application, and state that an alternative lacking the label is dropped (with `label.unset` WARN), never an error, in both extensional spellings.

### OWN-6 SUSPECTED (answer, doc; effort S; blast: occurrence counting, search results) Two `some`s with opposite identity rules (§2.6 vs §8 `occurs`)
Rule: §2.6 "Every `some(s)` over the same set in one predicate denotes the SAME occurrence (two occurrences need two names)". §8 `occurs.some(c)` is a FILTER, and filters combine independently under `&`.
Witness: `p := /(?:(?<n>\d),)+/` on `1,2,`: `p & occurs.some(n: 1) & occurs.some(n: 2)` — as two independent filters it MATCHES (iteration 1 is 1, iteration 2 is 2). The search spelling `s := $line ~ /\d/` with two conditions on `some(s)` (one `==1`, one `==2`) must NOT match (one occurrence cannot be both). Same word, same "there exists" reading, opposite sharing. Confirm by: the spec stating whether two `occurs.some` calls in one predicate may pick different iterations (the filter reading says yes; nothing says otherwise).
Recommendation: one rule for both: each `some(...)` APPLICATION picks its own witness (the filter reading, which is what `&` of filters means); a user wanting one occurrence satisfying two tests writes ONE application with the conjoined condition (`some(s & ==1 & ...)` / `occurs.some(n: (A & B))`). This also deletes the "two occurrences need two names" naming rule (a simplification).

### OWN-7 CONFIRMED (design, doc; effort S; blast: library signatures, member access) Bare words acting as keywords or reserved members, against "a word is always a NAME" (§7, §3.1-3.2, §5.3, §6.1; criterion 2)
Rule: §5.1 "NO ALPHABETIC OPERATORS OR STATEMENT KEYWORDS: a word is always a NAME (function, label, binding)" (OWNER RULED, Q6/Q9).
Witnesses:
- `records(text, rx, gaps = allow)` (§7): `allow` is not a function, label or binding; it is an enum keyword. Same for `sort(alts, by = length, desc)` (§7): `desc` is a bare flag word (and `by = length` passes the TRANSFORM `length` as a key, a third role for that name). Fix within the rule: a string or rx value (`gaps = "allow"`, `order = "desc"`), or separate library names (`sort_desc`).
- Metadata members `.index` and `.label` (§3.1, §5.3) share the dot namespace with user labels (§3.2 "`X.name` = LABEL navigation"). `r := /(?<index>\d+),(?<label>\w+)/`: `r.index` is the label `index` or the accept-state position? `x.label` in `capture(x) := /(?<${x.label}>${x})/` breaks for a user group named `label`. A word that is sometimes a user name and sometimes built-in syntax is the keyword problem in member position; the collision rule of the 2026-11-13 `$` SIGIL note (error naming both readings) is not extended here. Fix: a non-alphabetic metadata mark (spelling open), or an error for user labels named like a metadata member.
- Built-ins "reserved in CALL position only" (§5.1: `import`, `enumerate`, `pattern`, `some`, `all`) while `param`, `file_text`, `records`, `days`, `weekday_of`, `within` appear in examples unlisted; a user binding `some := ...` or `param(x) := ...` is either an error or shadows. Underdetermined: state the reserved list and that it is closed (or that built-ins are an implicitly imported module, `$`-free names like any other, shadowing WARNs as for labels).
- Not a defect, noted held: PCRE flag letters after a literal (`/.../x`, `/.../m`) are PCRE syntax inside the literal boundary (G3), not expression-language keywords.
- Unstated: how a program selects `functions`/`captures`/`vm`/`eval` (§6.1). If as bare words in program text (`@level(vm)`), they are keyword constants; as strings or API options they are fine. The spec should say which.

### OWN-8 CONFIRMED (answer via `.index`/`[]`, doc; effort S; blast: arrays built from bindings) A binding inserted in a literal is not its textual expansion, and a hole vs `|` give different arrays (§3.3, §3.5, §6.2)
Rule: §3.3 "a `${}` HOLE is ONE entry ... with `x := /a|b|c/`: `x | /d/` has 4; `/${x}|d/` has 2".
Witness: `x := /a|b|c/`; `t1 := /${x}|d/`; `t2 := x | /d/`; `t3 := /a|b|c|d/`. All three are ONE language (`&`-equal), yet `t1[2]` = `/d/`, `t2[2]` = `t3[2]` = `/b/`; on subject `d`, `t1 & ...` reports `.index` 2, `t2`/`t3` report 4 — and `.index` is a DEFAULT-LEVEL OUTPUT (§6.2 accept-class values), so this is a visible answer difference, not only structure. A user refactoring `t3` into `t1` ("name the first three") changes the reported index. The rule is stated, so this is CONFIRMED-as-designed; the finding is that it is a look-alike trap with no diagnostic.
Recommendation: when a structural read (`[]`, `.index`, `each`, `enumerate`) meets a hole-entry that is itself a multi-entry array, WARN (a named warning, e.g. `array.hole`) suggesting `|` to splice; list the trio in the spec's look-alike table.

### OWN-9 CONFIRMED (answer, doc; effort S; blast: brackets, E-SEP) Same-looking brackets and commas with opposite connectives (§3.1, §3.5, §13 C8, C5, C25; adds consequence)
Witnesses: `t & [(x: <200), (y: >55)]` = OR (array of filters, §3.5: `[f, g](t)` = `f(t) | g(t)`); `t & (x: <200, y: >55)` = AND (the comma record of §4.1/C8). One bracket shape changes the connective. Next, `x[[1, 3, 5]]` (positions 1, 3, 5: `[1,3,5]` = `1|3|5` is an integer set) vs `x & [1, 3, 5]` (the strings `1`, `3`, `5`) vs `x[1, 3, 5]` (C5: maybe positions, maybe a 2-D index). And C25: `lex_ge([major, minor, patch], [1, 4, 0])` passes an ARRAY that §3.1 says IS the alternation `1|4|0`, so the tuple order the macro needs is not part of the value it receives (an rx is extensional: `[1,4,0]` and `[0,1,4]` are `&`-equal). Recommendation: adopt predx C8's drop of the comma record (a `&` chain says AND already) and give tuples a non-array spelling or pass labels by name (`lex_ge(major = 1, minor = 4, patch = 0)`), so `[...]` means alternation everywhere.

### OWN-10 CONFIRMED (answer, doc; effort S; blast: class-derived arrays, E-SEP) `enumerate` order of a CLASS is not defined by "PCRE priority", yet E-SEP's expected `.index` depends on it (§5.5, §12.10, §3.4)
Rule: §5.5 entries "in PCRE PRIORITY ORDER (leftmost alternative, greedy before lazy)"; §3.4 "internal rewrites (altcls ...) never change user-visible structure".
Witness: `enumerate(/[-\/.]/)` vs `enumerate(/[.\/-]/)`: one language, one alternative each; a class has NO priority among its members, so the rule gives no order. E-SEP expects `2026/11/14` -> `.index` 2, i.e. WRITTEN order (`-`, `/`, `.`); codepoint order (`-` 0x2D, `.` 0x2E, `/` 0x2F) would give 3. Either spelling order (then two spellings of one class give different `.index`, and `[a-c]` vs `[cba]` differ) or codepoint order (then E-SEP's expectation is wrong). Recommendation: codepoint order for class members (extensional, spelling-independent), stated; fix E-SEP's expected index to 3 or reorder its class.

### OWN-11 CONFIRMED (answer, doc; effort S; blast: every field pipeline, every value-vs-value relation) Two more look-alike pairs already listed in §13, with consequences (C22, C13)
- C22: `isodate & (month: unpad & between(1, 12))` vs the unparenthesized `isodate & month: unpad & between(1, 12)` (the spelling of the owner-adopted pullback note itself). By §4.1 the second is `(month: unpad) & between(1, 12)`: a no-op condition, then the WHOLE `YYYY-MM-DD` string filtered by `between(1, 12)` = EMPTY: the program never matches. One pair of parentheses = a working validator vs a dead one; only a no-op warning points at it. Recommendation: `name:` takes the whole `&` chain to its right up to `|` or a closing parenthesis (a chain is one condition, as the pipeline reading requires), or an ERROR when the operand of `name:` is a bare transform.
- C13: `wname.index: ==wd` (E-RFC, bare binding as a value) vs `b: >$a` (§4.6, `$` value). Both compare a field with another match value; one needs `$`, one must not have it, and §4.1 says "`$x` is ALWAYS a value used". If `wd` is a computed label (§4.4: a match-dependent binding IS one), consistency asks `==$wd`; then `d: between(-15, 15)` (§12.7) and `==$wd` read the same kind of thing the same way. Recommendation: computed labels follow labels: `name:` to constrain, `$name` to read.

### OWN-13 CONFIRMED (doc, design; effort S; blast: the whole goal table) The owner's FIRST-ranked criterion is not a goal; nor are his other two ruled criteria (§0)
§0's ranked table (MANAGER DRAFT, "not yet seen by the owner") has G1 expressiveness > G2 linear default > G3 PCRE compatibility > G4 terminating structure. The owner's 2026-11-14 STANDING CRITERION ("ranked first for this design": look-alike consistency / intuitive behaviour) appears nowhere in §0, and the two other ruled design criteria (no alphabetic operators/keywords; `&` extensional) live only as rules in §2.2/§5.1. YES, this is a DOC finding, and a design one: with the criterion absent, the table's own tie-break ("highest wins a conflict") lets G1 (expressiveness) beat intuitiveness, the opposite of the owner's ranking (OWN-1's pipeline-on-`&` is exactly an expressiveness-for-look-alike trade the table would endorse). Recommendation: add G0 LOOK-ALIKE CONSISTENCY ranked above G1, with a checkable form: a LOOK-ALIKE TABLE in the spec (every pair of forms a reader would expect to agree, e.g. OWN-1..OWN-11's pairs, each marked SAME, or DIFFERENT + the named diagnostic the compiler emits when they differ for the program at hand); acceptance = no DIFFERENT row without a diagnostic. Record criteria 2 and 3 as constraints on every goal (rules, not ranked goals).

### OWN-14 CONFIRMED (design; effort S-M; blast: G3 and its check) G3 as stated is unattainable under the draft's own rules, and its check would fail on today's corpus (§0 G3, §11 strict subset, §6.1)
G3: "every PCRE pattern is a valid one-literal program with PCRE's meaning"; check: the whole `.rxt` corpus wrapped as `/.../` answers identically. But §11 makes the STRICT PURE SUBSET apply to literals ("refuse `\K`, `(*ACCEPT)`, `\G`, backtracking verbs, callouts/inline code; unbounded backreferences need `vm`") and §11 says the predicate opt-in selects the language, so a one-literal program IS a predicate program. Grep heuristic over `tests/**/*.rxt` (upper bounds, files containing): `\K` 37, `\G` 30, verbs 5, `(?C` 10, `\k<` 13, `\1-9` 70. Every such pattern is a refusal or needs `vm`, not "PCRE's meaning". §6.1's "Plain PCRE patterns are unchanged" either exempts one-literal programs from the subset (say so in §11) or is false. Also unstated in the check: `/` inside corpus patterns needs `\/` (wrapping is not identity), and .rxt flags must become per-literal flags (§11 "NO INHERITED FLAGS"). Recommendation: state G3 as "a one-literal program is EXACTLY today's PCRE pattern (no subset, no level)", and the strict subset applies only to literals inside a larger program; the check wraps with escaping and flag transfer.

### OWN-15 CONFIRMED (doc; effort S; blast: goal ranking) The ranking is not operative and two checks share a source with what they check (§0)
- §0 says the highest goal wins a conflict, then that G1 vs G2 conflicts "resolve by the capability levels ... not by dropping G1"; in practice the default level resolves EVERY G1/G2 conflict toward G2 (compile error, opt-in to regain G1). The order G1 > G2 is therefore never used; either rank G2 above G1 at the default level (what the rules do) or drop "highest wins".
- G1's check ("every §12 case in at most ~6 definitions") uses acceptance cases chosen by the same author who wrote the rules (a control sharing its source, learnings §3); "~6" is not a threshold. G2's check needs a build (`RX_ENGINE` stamp), so at design stage it is uncheckable; say "checked at build; at design: each §12 case names its level", which §12 already does. G4's check is fine (watchdog fuzz).
- Order served: the document's body serves G1 most (§§2-5, 8), G2 by §6 and §4.6, G3 by §11, G4 by §7; consistent with the table, but criterion 1 is served only incidentally (C-list), never as a goal.

## PROBED-AND-HELD

Rules examined: 41 RULE paragraphs in §§1-11 (all except §11's context-marked-sets model, Q1, left to other critics), the 11 §12 programs, §13 C1-C28/O1-O25 cross-checked for restatement. No compiler runs (no claim needed today's PCRE semantics); one grep over tests/ for OWN-14. Witnesses are derived from the draft's own definitions.

Held (look-alike pairs that DO behave alike, or differ visibly by design):
- `x & p` = `p(x)` for parameterless filters (§2.3); `p(a: s)` = `p & (a: s)` (§4.1); `isodate(year: 2000)` = `isodate & (year: 2000)` (§5.2): consistent.
- `!` is never `&!`; `A & !B` = `(!B)(A)` (§2.2): one meaning (but see OWN-2 on standalone use).
- `x:` vs `$x` (§4.1): a distinct-by-design pair with an error for the trap (`$x & ...` as a condition); holds.
- `"a.b"` vs `/a.b/`: different delimiters, stated difference; holds.
- `x[<4]` (positions) vs `x & <4` (values): different operators, `[]` errors on non-numeric filters; holds (spelled apart).
- Pure-rx `&` associativity/commutativity (membership) and `|` concatenating arrays: hold when no transform or hole is involved.
- Arrays of filters/transforms (§3.5): closure consistent with `|` on filters.
- `occurs.exactly/at_least/at_most` vs value comparisons: kept apart by the ruling (no number type); holds.
- Criterion 2: `where`, `each`, `join`, `when`, `some`, `all`, `occurs.*`, `assert.*`, `import`, `@export`, `@warn.*` are all function/member NAMES in call position, not operators; `@` and `~` are symbols. Held, apart from OWN-7's bare enum/flag words and metadata members.
- Criterion 3: `&`/`|`/`!` over rx are extensional in every rule examined; the breaks found are in what `&` is overloaded to do (transforms, OWN-1), what comparisons read (OWN-3) and structural side effects (C17, OWN-5, OWN-8).
