### TH-1 (design/doc, READ-CONFIRMED) The dense-hit slowdown is a default-ON loss that nobody is recorded as having judged
- Where: docs/spec/tuning.md 2.41p8c ("It can be slower ... 1.38 ms against 0.81 ms denied", about 1.7x on `[^@ ]+@x` under --engine=vm; 2.41p9 "Default: on"); plan.md [REQ-PAST-LAST] status ("dense hits cost more ... as the bounded row; cloud test OWED").
- The trade: a pattern/subject class (occurrences dense, every failed attempt passes one) gets slower by one pre-check re-ask per attempt; the win class is rare (8 corpus patterns at auto of 5,023, 155 under --engine=vm). The row is chosen by predicate, not priced: the DFA `handoff` row (2.41p8a) is gated by a byte-rate-prior inequality (`1/h - K > 1/m`); the VM attempt rows (p8b, p8c) have no such guard, so the speed given up is accepted by construction.
- Who judged: the spec states the fact and defends it with "which is why the deny is public" (consistent with the flag-follows-caller-effect rule), but there is no decisions.md row, no ruling reference, and the plan row records it as a status clause only. The rule that says a public deny is the remedy covers the FLAG, not the decision to ship a default that is 1.7x slower on a named class. The timings cite a lane report (not a durable spec/decision source), and the plan row defers the confirming measurement ("cloud test OWED") while the abi is already bumped and the spec says default on.
- Also: p8c reports that the BOUNDED `handoff-attempts` row has the same shape (1.56 vs 0.71 ms, about 2.2x on `[^@ ]{1,5}@x`). That is a pre-existing default-on loss newly discovered here; p8b (the bounded row's own paragraph) and its plan history do not say "it can be slower". A reader of p8b alone still believes it is free.
- Fix/effort: small. (a) a decisions row (or an explicit manager ruling cite in p8c) naming the accepted trade and the trigger that would revisit it (a bench cell where the dense class matters; or pricing, which the pricing-baseline direction defers); (b) one sentence in p8b pointing to p8c's measurement for the bounded row; (c) land the cloud/bench confirmation or mark the p8c numbers as the lane's own single-box best-of-seven in the spec text. Blast radius: doc only.

### TH-3 (maintenance, READ-CONFIRMED) `k >= PCREC_W_UNBOUNDED` now stands for "this is the unbounded row" at four emit sites plus the stamp, each re-deriving the row's identity from a number

A fifth such site is a copy-paste away, and the DFA-route caller of emit_req_landmark_move (pf_emit_handoff, `f` non-NULL) shares the early-return with a VM-only case.
- Wrong table semantics too: the early-return sits AFTER `round` is built by pcrec_emit_start_zero and is then freed unused (wasted work and an ordering trap if start_zero ever grows a side effect); the header comments of emit_req_landmark_move and pcrec_emit_req_attempt_reentry ("moved to the hit less K", "nothing unless FIRST's `handoff-attempts` row keeps the whole run") were not updated and are now stale (the second is wrong for the new row; the first omits the no-move case).

Proposed form: a CandUse field (`.u.use.moves`/`.nomove`, sibling of `.whole` and `.reenter`, which the new row already sets) and one accessor `req_handoff_moves(cx)` read by the four emit sites and the stamp (`"unbounded"` follows the row's tok, not a K compare). Effort about 20 lines, gives deletion of the number-compare idiom; no abi change (emitted text identical). Blast radius: emit_dfa.c only; run make test-codegen (abi readers untouched).
- Minor, no side-effect hazard found in the predicates: req_handoff_vm_run copies CandSel and calls CAND_BOUND_ONE, req_admit_read(cx, ...) (existing pattern, pure by reading); held.

### TH-4 (design, SUSPECTED) The new row is the bounded row with the move deleted: a table row split by a numeric regime, where one row plus a per-row "move" field would do
- handoff-attempts-unbounded shares every conjunct but `K < UNBOUNDED` (now factored as req_handoff_vm_run) and sets the same `.u.use` flags as handoff-attempts. The stop-past-last property is NOT new in the unbounded row: the bounded `handoff-attempts` re-entry already answers NOMATCH when the re-ask misses; what K=infinity changes is only that `c - K` is never ahead. So the lane found that the K<UNBOUNDED conjunct of handoff-attempts was an over-restriction (the move is a no-op, not a reason to decline) and encoded the repair as a separate row with a separate stamp "unbounded" and a separate abi step, instead of dropping the conjunct and letting the move emit nothing (TH-3's field). Cost of the split: two rows to keep in step in desc strings, witnesses, sabotage rows (S1955-S1957 duplicate S1704/S1706's logic), a third deny-site test list, and the stamp special case. The split is defensible if the user-visible stamp must differ ("unbounded" vs digits), but then that is a stamp rule, not a row.
- Effort medium (merge two rows, retarget three sabotages); only worth doing if TH-3's field is adopted. Raise as a consideration for the manager, not a blocker.

### TH-7 (the two standing questions)
- Third member of a family? YES. The `req-use` table's handoff rows are one family keyed on (route, K regime, re-entry): `handoff` (DFA/hybrid, finite K), `handoff-attempts` (VM, finite K, re-entry), `handoff-attempts-entry` (VM, finite K, entry only), now `handoff-attempts-unbounded` (VM, K unbounded, re-entry, no move). That is already four rows with the predicate conjuncts restated or factored by hand. The asker's "third member" is the K-regime axis: finite-entry, finite-reentry, unbounded-reentry.
- General form: ONE statement of the fact "a necessary whole run is held at or after every attempt start" plus three orthogonal declared capabilities per route: (1) ENTRY GATE (first hit; exists everywhere), (2) MOVE (start := hit minus K; needs finite K; row field `.moves`), (3) STOP (re-ask when the attempt passes the kept hit; a miss ends the search; needs linearity i.e. parts_in_whole on VM, or the forward scan at its start state on DFA). The rows then are the cross product that the predicates can support, and the stamp reads the row. The unbounded row is exactly {entry, stop, no move}.
- Unlocks with triggers (D77: each waits for a measured cell, none filed as a build):
  1. DFA/hybrid unbounded STOP: `handoff` (2.41p8a) declines `k >= UNBOUNDED`, but the forward scan at its start state past the last occurrence could end likewise. Trigger: a bench cell whose RX_REQ_HANDOFF "none" DFA artifact has a long no-match tail. This is the missing sibling of this row (the VM-only restriction is the largest unserved share of the 250-pattern population, TH-2).
  2. Single-required-byte STOP: the row needs `whole_len >= 2`; a one-byte landmark (`[^@ ]+@`) at unbounded K is the plain gate-plus-stop and uses the same re-ask. Trigger: corpus census of unbounded single-byte landmarks (the facts lane's count by `req_byte_stated`).
  3. `-entry` unbounded: declined by design (entry-only unbounded is the plain gate, p8c text). No unlock.
  4. Pricing: the dense-hit loss (TH-1) is the case for the pricing baseline; when pricing lands, the stop row gets the same `1/h` inequality the DFA row has. Trigger: pricing is built (deferred); until then record the accepted trade.
  5. Cross-engine (stream roadmap): the stop test is "the last occurrence", which a reverse find or the cross-engine reverse DFA states directly in one call instead of re-asking forward; trigger: A-REV/reverse-DFA landing, then the kit owns the method (D157) and the re-ask loop could be replaced.
