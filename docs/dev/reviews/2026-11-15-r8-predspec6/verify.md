### V1 hole guard (OWNER-4 + RUL-1 + CENSUS-10): HOLDS
Read: §5.1 L634-635 "a macro with only its subject open (a condition, a section) is a guard (§11)" (in the sentence on "use in a hole", no `&` mentioned); §2.2 L253-254 "A standalone `!B` where an rx is needed (`${!B}`, `C | !B`) is the unapplied-macro error"; §0.1 L21 ("`${!<200}` the unapplied-macro error"); §11 L1055-1057 "a GUARD is written with an explicit `&`: `(\d+)&${ <256 }`; a hole after an atom WITHOUT `&` is a FOLLOWING item"; CF:362 "not usable as `${x: digit}`". Per §2.1 L213 `!B` is "a macro with the subject open", so §5.1 and §2.2 give two answers for `${!B}` (guard vs error); §11's guard holds a full rx and needs `&`. Charitable reading (guard = `&${macro}`) is not what §5.1 writes.

### V2 `[ ]` any-one vs ordered + tuple reason (OWNER-3 + CENSUS-1 + RUL-5 + SIM-6): PARTLY
HOLDS: the stated reason is self-contradicting. §8 L955-956: "passed tuples as values, which the type picture forbids: a `[ ]` is an alternation (any one entry, so its order is not part of the value) and a record is never a value (CF:863, CF:866)"; §3.4 L462-464 "STRUCTURAL (indexing, `each`, `join`...) read the SPELLING: `/a|b/` and `/b|a/` ... differ under `[1]`"; §12 L1098-1099 admits `[ ]` "passed to a structural macro that reads its entries in order (`join`)". CF:866 (4) forbids RECORDS ("A RECORD is NOT a value"), not arrays; the tuple claim is only the manager's "TO CHECK ... not ruled" (CF:876-877); CF:548 says "join takes an ordinary array, no tuple type". C25 L1398 repeats "the type picture forbids".
REFUTED in part: CENSUS-1/OWNER-3's "two meanings of `[ ]`" overstates; §3.4 gives one meaning (an rx whose spelling is ordered; extensional ops see the alternation, structural ops the entries). OWNER-3's own fix direction states exactly that. Defect is the L955 reason and the C25/O47 motivation, not a second bracket meaning.

### V13 destructuring loses pairing (RUL-8): HOLDS

### V14 statement-start parse ambiguity (CENSUS-14): HOLDS
§5.1 L630 "newlines are layout (whitespace and newlines are free between expression tokens)"; §2.5 L328-329 "a name after an expression always starts a binding (one-token look-ahead)" (covers a NAME only); §5.1 L633 / §2.6 L341 statement `(x, y) := ...` starts with `(`; `[` starts a final array expression. `(` and `[` after an expression are postfix (call/index, §5.2 L669, §3.2). So `d := $m ~ /x/` NEWLINE `(x, y) := ...` or `t := foo` NEWLINE `[a, b]` parse as call/index. The destructuring half is design-only (§0.2); the `[`-final-expression half is live.

### V15 `[a, b]` vs `a | b` structural difference (CENSUS-2): PARTLY

### V16 section lift vs identity (CENSUS-9): PARTLY
§2.1 L211-213 "a CONDITION ... = a macro with a subject and no parameters; ... an operator SECTION `(& B)` = only the subject open"; §2.3 L290-291 / §0.1 L6 (line 92) require `each((& (a: d)), x)`; §5.3 L711-713 "f is a MACRO whose parameters the entry fills ..., or a FILTER passed as an operator SECTION". HOLDS: `(& (a: d))` and `(a: d)` coincide extensionally, `each((a: d), x)` falls between the two forms f may take (a condition has no parameters), and §0.1 has no row for it. REFUTED: "two meanings for one mark". `(& B)` has one meaning (x maps to `x & B`); for an rx B that is a lift, for a subject-open macro B it is the identity, a consequence not a second meaning.
