## Findings
### PRE-1 grid2's `streamgen.py` does not implement the window rule or the disjointness check the design names
- status: CONFIRMED
- severity: design
- effort: medium
- blast radius: H and D windows can share text. The "disjointness" the design relies on for leakage protection (§3.2 item 2) and for the D/H strata split (§5.2) would pass while about half of all adjacent window pairs overlap in pools below about 8 MiB. That is every pool §5.2 names as small, and every pool where M = floor(U / 64 KiB).
- where: subject §5.1 table row "streamed windows" ("`streamgen.py`: 64 disjoint 64 KiB windows per subject, with a `disjoint` check across subjects (the §3.2 item 2 mechanism)"); §3.2 item 2 ("4 KiB chunk hash and byte range ... with grid2's `streamgen.py disjoint` as the mechanism"); §5.2 ("drawn by its stratified rule ... trimmed to whole lines, made by `streamgen.py`"); and "What it buys: ... the streamed windows with their disjointness check". Code: `docs/dev/lanes/grid2/streamgen.py:90-113`; regime_eval.md:948-960 (the rule), :1407-1420 (the E6 proof). Relates to X15/X26.
- finding: (a) `disjoint()` (streamgen.py:90-99) compares the sha256 of WHOLE buffers only. It does no 4 KiB chunk hashing and no byte-range test, so two windows that share 60 KiB of text but differ by one byte read as "disjoint". The chunk/byte-range proof is regime_eval's E6 (regime_eval.md:1407-1420), which is not built in grid2. (b) `window_offsets()` (streamgen.py:100-113) jitters each window over `room = min(span, size-n+1-j*span)`, so a window can run past its own stratum into the next one. regime_eval's rule keeps each window inside its stratum: `mod (U/M - 64 KiB + 1)` (regime_eval.md:950-951). So streamgen is not "its stratified rule". (c) streamgen has no pool concatenation, no exemplar reserve and no trimming to whole lines (grep: no line logic in streamgen.py). §5.2's "made by `streamgen.py`" therefore needs a new generator (the E4 rule), and the new-work list and §11 do not include one. They list only the SHORT generator.
- repro: `work/overlap.py` copies `window_offsets` verbatim and runs 200 salts per pool size. Pools of 0.4 / 1.4 / 2.0 / 4.0 MiB: 54 / 51 / 52 / 51 % of adjacent window pairs overlap, by about 22 KiB on average. 8 MiB: 13 %. 64 MiB: 0 %. Expected under regime_eval's rule: 0 % at every size (`work/overlap.out`).
- proposed fix: Add to the new-work list and to §11 a regime_eval-rule window generator (inside-stratum jitter, reserve, line trim, manifest of (file, offset, length)) and the E6 chunk + byte-range proof. Make the D/H strata check read byte ranges, not buffer shas. Correct the §5.1 row and the "what it buys" sentence.

### PRE-2 The X26 fix does not hold: grid2's quiet gate also proceeds after 20 min, and its re-queue missed three of the four load excursions in grid2's own timed run
- status: CONFIRMED
- severity: design
- effort: medium
- blast radius: a contaminated group is graded silently. A +4..+11 % excursion on some arms of one group (grid2read's measured effect) is many times the planned NULL_i of about 1 point, so it can move a cell oracle or a site oracle, and therefore a regret.
- where: subject §5.1 table row "a loaded group" (grid1 "waits up to 20 min, then PROCEEDS (X26)" against grid2 "re-queues a group whose load breaches `LOADBREACH` (3.0) and re-runs it"); "What it buys: ... the re-queue (X26)"; "Quiet: grid2's gate and re-queue, plus the per-core check". Code: `docs/dev/lanes/grid2/time.sh:152` (the start wait: 60 x 20 s, then it runs anyway, exactly grid1's loop), `:210-213` (re-queue only when the END load1 >= 3.0, once; a second breach is KEPT and marked `BREACH(twice)`), `:221-226`. Evidence: `docs/dev/lanes/grid2read_report.md:33-36, :94-106`.
- finding: The table presents "proceeds after 20 min" as a grid1-only defect. grid2's start gate is the same loop, so the grid1 cell is unfair and X26's first half is not fixed. The re-queue looks at load1 once, at group end, against 3.0, which is twice the gate's 1.5. grid2's real slot (2026-10-16) had four excursions. The harness caught one (`f3-dig`, load1 3.12). It neither logged nor re-queued the other three: `f3-word-eq` n1m at load1 2.08 (walk 1.107x), `f3-quad` n64k at 2.76 (+4..+11 % in launches 1-2), F1 `dig g8 L1k u @7` at 1.70. The reader found them after the fact and invented a LOAD-SUSPECT launch-exclusion rule for them (grid2read §2). grid2read also says "the one-minute average lags, so a row's load1 does not certify the row". The new per-core check runs "at every group boundary", so it inherits the same blind spot: a competitor that starts and stops inside a group, which lasts about 55 s at 17 launches per round x 5 x 0.65 s.
- repro: read time.sh:152 (`for t in $(seq 1 "$LOADWAIT") ... sleep 20; done` with no fail branch) and :210 (`l1 >= LOADBREACH`); grid2read_report.md:94-106 (the table of the three unlogged visits, "the harness logged none of them").
- proposed fix: Record per-core occupancy per LAUNCH (not per group boundary). Pre-register a launch-exclusion and re-time rule (grid2read's LOAD-SUSPECT, made mechanical) with a stop when a group still breaches after its re-time. Restate the table row so grid1 and grid2 both read "proceeds after 20 min".

### PRE-4 The rerun share and the launch cost are not what grid2 measured, and the "+10 %" does not follow even from the design's own figures
- status: CONFIRMED
- severity: speed
- effort: small
- blast radius: stage 2 is underpriced by roughly 1-3 h of the 12 h, more if the cross-launch trigger scales as modelled. The jobs-of-an-hour plan and the box calendar are sized on the wrong figure. The verdict itself is not affected.
- where: subject §5.1 table row "cost per launch" ("0.61-0.65 s per streamed 4 MiB launch, MEASURED by its DRY run"); "What grid2 costs us" item (1) ("about 20 % of groups take 2 more launches, the slot's EP69 estimate), about +10 % of stage 2 in all"); §5.6 ("A launch is 0.65 s (grid2's DRY-run measurement, X28) ... 5.4 launches"); §0 item 7 ("re-derived at grid2's measured 0.65 s"). Sources: `docs/dev/lanes/grid2/time.sh:55-56`; `grid2r3_report.md:24,112,130,159`; `grid2p_report.md:39,51`; `grid2read_report.md:15,68-70`; regime_eval.md:1811 (EP69 "CHOSEN (an estimate; the realised share is reported)").
- finding: (a) grid2's full TIMED slot has run (COMPLETE 2026-10-16 20:06, grid2read_report.md:15). Its realised rerun share is F1 32/123, F3 5/16, OB 26/60, TRIG 3/12, which is 66/211 = 31 %. That was at LAUNCHES = 3 with about 9 launches per round. The design still uses EP69's chosen 20 %. Both triggers (time.sh:169-182) test ANY launch and ANY arm, so the share grows with 5 launches and 13-17 arms. `work/rerun_sim.py` gives about 30 % if the noise is per group and 60-86 % if it is per launch (tuned to 31 % at k = 9, L = 3). grid2read says the cross-launch trigger fired in 28 of 32 F1 reruns, which points at the per-launch case. (b) The 0.61-0.65 s comes from time.sh's comment. That DRY ran at box load 12-17, "not a measurement" (grid2r3_report.md:24). The lane reports give 0.55-0.69 s (r3) and 0.62-0.74 s (p, F1). (c) Arithmetic: base#c is 17/16 = +6.25 % and the rerun share is 5.4/5 = +8 %, so the total is 1.0625 x 1.08 = +14.75 %, not "about +10 %". At the measured 31 % it is +19 %. (d) The grid1 cell in the same row quotes grid1's REPEATED-subject cost. grid1's streamed cost is `SEC_STREAM`, and the same driver on the same 4 MiB subjects costs the same, so the row does not distinguish the two harnesses.

- proposed fix: Price stage 2 from grid2's real slot (0.67-0.71 s, a rerun share of at least 31 %, scaled to 5 launches and 17 arms, or `--estimate` on a dry census of the bake-off's own arms). Restate item (1) as about +15-20 % and §5.6/§11 accordingly. Drop "the slot's EP69 estimate".

### PRE-5 Launch cost depends on arm speed, and grid2's arms were all fast scans
- status: SUSPECTED
- severity: speed
- effort: small
- blast radius: stage-2 hours. The L-ROW no-search arm (about 65 % of sites) and the VM-engine arms may be much slower per byte than grid2's arms.
- where: subject §5.6 ("A launch is 0.65 s"). `docs/dev/lanes/grid1/gdrv.c:74` (an answer pass over EVERY buffer before timing), `:84-86` (doubling calibration to at least 50 ms, then 7 loops).
- finding: A launch costs one full answer pass over all M windows, plus calibration, plus 7 loops of at least 50 ms. When one 64 KiB pass takes more than about 1 ms (below about 65 MB/s), the answer pass and the at-least-2-pass loops dominate. At 10 ms per pass a launch is about 1.3 s (64 x 10 ms + about 140 ms + 7 x 80 ms). grid2's 0.6-0.7 s measured only memchr/V1/walk-class scans.
- repro: what would confirm it: `time.sh --estimate` (or one DRY launch) on a census sample that includes the no-search and VM arms.
- proposed fix: Take §5.6's seconds per launch from the stage-0 dry census per arm class, not from grid2's constant.

### PRE-6 C7's round halves share every launch, so they cannot see the dominant noise in grid2's data (launch-level bimodality)
- status: SUSPECTED
- severity: design
- effort: small
- blast radius: C7 (and C7-FM, which gates row 1) can PASS while the oracle choice is driven by launch-level noise. That is the noise grid2's own cross-launch trigger exists for (it fired in 28 of 32 F1 reruns and 23 of 26 OB reruns). The control "the oracle is noise" then cannot fail on the noise most likely to be present.
- where: subject §5.4 ("C7 splits ROUNDS ... each launch's 7 rounds split into odd and even"); §8 C7 row; the TSV rows C7 and C7-FM. `docs/dev/lanes/grid1/gdrv.c:86` (the 7 rounds are consecutive loops in ONE process); grid2/time.sh:13-18 (why the cross-launch trigger exists); grid2read_report.md:70,128-130. Round 2's X14.
- finding: Rounds of one launch share the process, its layout, its frequency state and its cache placement. A launch-level effect (gridread/grid2read: the walk is "bimodal ACROSS launches"; per-launch spread above 5 % in 7 of 83 find-all groups) moves every round of that launch together, so it is identical in the odd and even halves. C7 (a) and (b) then compare two copies of the same launch noise and agree. Cross-fit's LAUNCH halves are the split that sees this noise, and the design computes them but never tests their agreement.
- repro: what would confirm it: in the drill or a simulation, add a per-launch effect with σ_L much larger than σ_round and check that C7(b)'s disagreement share stays at the σ_round level while the launch-half oracles disagree.
- proposed fix: Add a C7(c): the site oracle's agreement between the two cross-fit LAUNCH halves, with a bar derived on D like `c7b_bar`, and a NOT-RUN/red row in the file.

### PRE-7 Timing definitions that do not match what grid2 records: medians of even counts, and the raw file's `round` column
- status: CONFIRMED
- severity: doc
- effort: small
- blast radius: an implementer using grid2's `med()` biases the larger half of every rerun group (and every odd round-half) low. A scorer reading `raw_*.tsv`'s `round` column as the design's "round" splits launches where C7 wants rounds.
- where: subject §5.4 ("a TWO-launch half takes their MEAN ... A rerun group's halves are the odd and even launches of all seven"; "rounds split into odd (1, 3, 5, 7) and even (2, 4, 6)"); §5.1 "Raw per-launch AND per-round values are kept (`time/raw_<PHASE>.tsv` plus the per-round print)". Code: `docs/dev/lanes/grid2/time.sh:140` (`med` = `a[int((NR+1)/2)]`, the lower middle for even NR), `:38,130,147` (the raw column named `round` is the LAUNCH index i; "round > LAUNCHES is a rerun round"); grid1/gdrv.c:87-88 (qsort in place, then prints t[3]: round order is gone before any print). X14.
- finding: X14 fixed the median of TWO only. The rerun group's odd half {1,3,5,7} has FOUR launches, and C7's odd round-half has FOUR rounds. grid2's `med()` takes the lower middle for both, which is the bias X14 removed for two. In grid2's raw TSV, `round` means launch. And K9's per-round hook has to print before `gdrv.c:87`'s in-place sort.
- repro: `med "1 2 3 4"` returns 2 (NR = 4, a[int(5/2)] = a[2]), where the median is 2.5.
- proposed fix: Define the median of any even count as the mean of the two middle values, computed from the raw file. Name grid2's `round` column as the launch index. Have K9 print each round in loop order before the sort.

### PRE-8 "regime_eval's NULL_c, literally" is not literal, formula by formula
- status: CONFIRMED
- severity: doc
- effort: small
- blast radius: a reader who checks the claim finds four differences. Item (i) means H-core's floor rests on the assumption that D's noise carries over to another window (grid2read found the noise floor split by regime, 11.6x, find-all against single calls). The verdict changes only if D's and H-core's noise differ.
- where: subject §0 Words ("NULL_c ... regime_eval's, literally"); §5.4 ("regime_eval's formula literally (§1.3, EP30)"; "Cross-fit splits LAUNCHES (regime_eval §4.3 "Winner's curse")"); §5.1 "What it buys: regime_eval's NULL_c literally (X13)". regime_eval.md:540-544, :1795 (EP30 "per slot"), :466-469 and :2120-2121 (per-arm A/A), :2105-2108 (C7: "cross-fit, NULL and ν from that half"), :529-532 (cross-fit = leave-one-launch-out, b(−l)). X13.
- finding: (i) regime_eval's ν is "the SLOT's own" median over that slot's groups. The design derives ν on D and applies it to H-core, which is timed in other windows. (ii) regime_eval uses a repeated arm's own A/A for differences involving it. The design scales every cell by the base's A/A and `ν · t_base` only, even between two non-base arms that may be several times slower. (iii) regime_eval's C7 recomputes NULL and ν inside each half. The design fixes ν from D. (iv) regime_eval's cross-fit is leave-one-launch-out, averaged over every l. The design's is a 2-fold odd/even split, but it cites §4.3, which points back to §1.3's leave-one-out. The max(AA_c, ν·t) formula itself matches.
- repro: read the cited regime_eval lines against subject §5.4's bullets.
- proposed fix: Replace "literally" with "regime_eval's formula, with ν pooled on D per (box, shape), no per-arm A/A, and a 2-fold cross-fit". Either add a check that H-core's own ν (from its base#c) is within EP31-style bounds of D's, or recompute ν on H-core.
