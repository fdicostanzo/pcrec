## Answers to the round's three questions

1. **X2 (one scale): the PAIR fix holds; three thresholds are still argued on another scale.** For a
   pair the site oracle cancels exactly (d = m_X − m_Y), so no pair test can order a pair differently
   from the regret the margin is argued on; round 2's FM-normalisation flip cannot recur (3 cases).
   But the large-loss bar (`big_loss_bp`, "1.5x", PR O1) is read as regret > 0.5 and is a 1.5x ratio
   only where the site has no compile-time choice loss: a member with the 1.5x losses can WIN the tail
   (Y2, answer-risk). δ's rationale sits on PR G1's total-time scale while every test reads the
   site-equal mean (Y3, disclosed via F3). C-BLIND's beat bar mixes a cell time with a ratio (Y4).
2. **X25 (drill and file): no per-scenario special-casing, thresholds read from the file, but the
   drill still decides one thing the file does not declare, and the checker guards only the rendered
   block.** All 45 scenarios go through `verdict()` → `first_match` on the file's rows; an
   independent evaluator agrees on 42 of 45; the 3 that differ are because the drill opens the frame
   whenever a scenario supplies sites, while the text opens it only for an UNDERPOWERED H-core pair
   (Y1). The checker caught 1 of 10 own plants (checker + drill: 4 of 10); prose δ = 300 against the
   file's 200 reads GREEN (Y5). Power re-derives: EQUIV n80 ≈ 44, 0.82-0.84 at 50, so
   `stage0_floor` = 50 holds; the 18-20 differing-site winner threshold holds (21 at s = 5 %), but
   only at CV = 1 (≈ 8 at CV = 0, ≈ 60 at CV = 2), so §0 item 1 needs its CV beside it. Tail: 9 own
   cases land as intended, but G_tail is non-transitive, so TAIL-WIN can crown a member that never
   measurably beat a rival (Y7, answer); and on H-core the tail can decide only at 6-7 one-way losses,
   since more losses widen the mean's interval into UNDERPOWERED (Y6).
3. **The grid decision: the choice may still stand, but §5.1's case for it is overstated in four
   places.** grid2's `streamgen.py` lacks the stratified window rule and the chunk/byte-range
   disjointness proof the design names (~50 % of adjacent windows overlap below 8 MiB; Y9). grid2's
   quiet gate proceeds after 20 min exactly as grid1's does, and its end-of-group re-queue missed 3 of
   4 load excursions in grid2's own slot (Y10). grid2's build, leg P and leg M assert the withdrawn V1
   (`pf_terms_mask`) design and fail at any main-line H commit, so the correctness gate needs an
   unlisted wrapper over grid1's check.sh (Y11). Costs are low: realised rerun share 31 %, not EP69's
   20 %; 0.67-0.71 s/launch measured on the real slot; "+10 %" is +15-20 % (Y12). What grid2
   demonstrably buys after this is `base#c` and the rerun rule; the manager may want rev 3 to re-weigh
   "grid1 + base#c + the rerun rule" against "grid2 minus P/M/streamgen", on the merits, in the table.

## Lessons for the guide (proposed)

1. **A declared file needs a sensitivity drill, not only a render check.** The checker verified that
   the rendered block equals the file; two params (`sup_level`, `big_loss_bp`) could change with no
   scenario moving, and prose could restate δ freely. Proposed critics.md lesson / simulate-lens text:
   "when a design declares its rule as data, perturb each parameter and each row order in a copy and
   ask (a) does some drill scenario move, (b) does the checker see the prose that restates it".
2. **A harness's choice table compares claims, not code.** Three §5.1 cells credited grid2 with
   behaviour its scripts do not have (stratified windows, a non-proceeding gate, legs that apply at
   our commit). Proposed premise-lens addition: "for every 'X has Y' cell in a choice table, cite the
   file:line in X that does Y, and the line in the alternative that lacks it".
3. **A threshold named by a ratio but read on a difference is a scale defect even after the pair
   statistics are unified.** X2's fix was scoped to pairs; per-unit thresholds kept their old scale.
   Proposed: the simulate lens's scale census covers every per-unit threshold, not only the pair test.
