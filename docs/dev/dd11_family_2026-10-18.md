### 1.4 What DD-11.5 means today (my reading, for Frank to rule)
Not "wire the whole table". Sets and tags are wired. What is left is the ASSERTION-family TEXT entries: `$`, `\Z`,
`(?m)^`, `(?m)$` (and the possessive suffix builder, already a hand-built equivalent). Per entry the exactness of the
substitution decides whether it can ship, because only single-character-language lookarounds are exact in the DFA
today:
- `(?m)$` = `(?=\n)|\z`: `(?=\n)` is one A_CTX, `\z` is core. Exact now.
- `(?m)^` = `\A|(?<=\n)(?!\z)`: `(?<=\n)` is A_CTX, but `(?!\z)` has body `\z` and `lang_charset` declines `A_END`, so
  it stays `A_LOOK` and the DFA erases it (the exact "U11b carve-out" conjunct D66 names). NOT exact today.
- `$` / `\Z` = `(?=\n?\z)`: body is not a single-character language. NOT exact today.
So three of four entries need either [ENG-LOOK] or an extension of the A_CTX recognizer (a two-sided node that reads
"next character exists" for `(?!\z)`). That extension is the same object as Frank's new customer, below.

### 1.5 The new customer: Frank's position-dependent `\b` rewrite
Source: `docs/design/kit_proof.md` §3.2, §8.4 Q3 (PENDING Frank's ruling; panel leaning: read-not-lower, DFA-only,
UL-6 BOONIES) and `docs/dev/reviews/2026-10-18-r3-kit-proof-u1.md` (k3-family Q3 / UL-6; fam-Q3). The idea: `\b`
followed by a word character is `(?<!\w)` (the next-character fact makes the lookahead half redundant). Two halves:
1. Definitions substitution: `\b` -> its table definition (needs the text entry consumed, 1.2).
2. A next-character fold: intersect the A_CTX next-side set with the FIRST set of the following element, so the node
   reduces to a one-sided test. Recognized into A_CTX, per the brief.
Facts relevant to the review:
- The kit proof currently READS the same fact off the anchored DFA's seed table (stage 1, DFA-only). A fold at the
  tree level is the "facts-layer derivation" kit_proof §3.2 files for U1b and UL-8, and it is what makes the fact
  available to VM-only patterns. Both consume the A_CTX node.
- Cost recorded against a tree rewrite in kit_proof §3.2: it moves every engine's bytes and the `--emit-ir` listing
  on every `\b` artifact for no answer change; "a second spelling of what the seed table encodes".
- TENSION TO RESOLVE: `plan.md` [ENG-LOOK] records Frank's ruling "NO one-character fold ships anywhere — it is the
  duplicate path". A_CTX (UD-2, D130) was later ruled as the general node, so the fold must be framed as the general
  form (FIRST-set intersection over any A_CTX or lookaround against its neighbour), not a `\b` special case, or it
  re-meets that ruling (memory `pcrec-general-mechanisms-not-special-cases`).
- The same fold (prev-set x next-set) is what would make `(?m)^`'s `(?!\z)` exact (1.4), so one mechanism could serve
  both customers; that is a design-review question, not a finding.

### 1.6 What a design review must re-decide
1. Scope: which entries are in 11.5 (sets are done). Candidates: `(?m)$` (exact now), `(?m)^`, `$`/`\Z` (need
   extended recognition or [ENG-LOOK]), possessive builder.
2. Substitute-as-text-then-recognize vs normalize-to-node (UD-2) as the model, now that `\b` took the node route. If
   node-normalization is the answer, what is the table's text for? (documentation and oracle leg, or a compile input)
3. Where exactness comes from: extend A_CTX (two-sided, "next exists") vs wait for [ENG-LOOK]. Frank's one-character-fold
   ruling governs the answer.
4. Whether `(?m)^`/`(?m)$` stop being `A_BOL`/`A_EOL` with a flag (D62's field+fold) and what the identity gate pins
   (byte-identical vs measured-equivalent; abi bump and readers by grep).
5. Where the stamp/recognizer discipline lives (S3 restated for A_CTX) and its sabotage row.
6. Whether the fold of 1.5 is part of 11.5 or its own row, and its relation to kit_proof Q3 / UL-6 / UL-8 / U1b.
7. [DD-11.6]'s fate (section 2) and the stale D66 premise that tranche-C waits on this reduction.
