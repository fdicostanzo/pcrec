## Self-audit (findings the main session found on itself, before the panel)

These are recorded first because they are the most important results of this
checkpoint and none of them came from a critic. They came from tooling built
during the same session, which is the one genuinely encouraging fact here.

- **R3-SELF-1** (CONFIRMED, FIXED) **M2.12 shipped a 43% regression.** The
  accept/EOL evaluation was moved after scan avoidance UNCONDITIONALLY. That
  is required under EOL and merely correct without it — and without it, it
  hoists the memchr prefilter above the accept check: `[01]*1[01]{8}` went
  158.4 → 90.8 MB/s (spreads 1.01–1.02x). Now conditional on `eol`.
  *Caught by:* the M2.11 compare gate, one commit after it was written.

- **R3-SELF-2** (CONFIRMED, REVERTED) **M2.10's skip-eligibility change was a
  27% regression recorded as a 40% improvement.** The "before" sample read
  83.7 MB/s where the true value is ~158 — un-interleaved, taken at load 1.69,
  never repeated. Two independent harnesses now agree.
  *Caught by:* generating the M2.11 gate's floors and noticing case (f) at
  118.6 against R2's published 159.1.

- **R3-SELF-3** (CONFIRMED, FIXED) **A false claim reached a commit message,
  D11, and the journal**: "non-EOL output is byte-identical across 8 probe
  patterns" was true of an earlier draft of M2.12 and false of what was
  committed, because the identity check was not re-run after the final change.
  It is true now and re-verified.

- **R3-SELF-4** (CONFIRMED, CORRECTED) **D13's "there is no crossover" between
  computed-goto and table dispatch was wrong.** The crossover is not in DFA
  size but in transition PREDICTABILITY: on predictable input computed goto is
  3.5x FASTER (ratio 0.28), on random input 2.5x slower. The decision stands;
  the stated reason was misleading. Also: the micro-benchmark's absolute
  numbers are untrustworthy (table reads 7234 MB/s at -O1 vs 350 at -O2), so
  only within-level ratios are meaningful.

- **R3-SELF-5** (CONFIRMED, FIXED) **`.gitignore`'s unanchored `core` matched
  the DIRECTORY `src/core/`**, so `arena.c`, `compile.c`, `sb.c` and
  `internal.h` — the entire pipeline driver — were never committed. **A fresh
  clone of the public repo did not build, and had not since M0.** Both R1 and
  R2 missed it because both reviewed the working tree rather than a clean
  clone. Verified fixed: `git clone && make && make test` now passes
  607/41/17.

- **R3-SELF-6** (CONFIRMED, FIXED) **The M2.9 budgets flake on a busy box.**
  Probing whether budgets tight enough to catch a 1.75x regression are also
  tight enough for load to fail them: yes. At 1-minute load 24.4 on 12 cores a
  known-good build measured 8572 MB/s on case (b) against its 12000 floor, and
  per-trial spreads widened from 1.15–1.35x to 1.38–1.53x. The tempting fix —
  loosen the budget — would have undone M2.9 within a week. Instead the suite
  now declines to judge above `LOAD_LIMIT` (default `max(2.0, cores/2)`): a
  miss becomes INCONCLUSIVE, is not counted, and the summary states that the
  run gated nothing. A PASS under load is still a PASS.

- **R3-SELF-7** (CONFIRMED, OPEN) **The codegen checks assert skip-table
  PRESENCE, not COUNT.** Changing `pick_skip_states`' cap from 4 to 1 — a
  plausible "simplification" — leaves all 17 codegen checks green, along with
  the corpus, the python oracle and the fuzzer. `make bench` did report budget
  failures on that build, but that measurement was taken at load >20 and the
  healthy build failed a budget in the same window, so **whether bench
  reliably catches it is UNVERIFIED** and should be re-measured on a quiet
  box. **SETTLED by the process critic:** no measurement was possible — all
  four bench patterns emit ZERO skip tables, and generated code is
  byte-identical with `pick_skip_states` returning 0. The cap of 4 also buys
  nothing measurable (730.8 vs 740.1 MB/s on the only case that uses skip
  states, interleaved x9), so asserting a COUNT would pin a number no
  measurement supports. The real gap is that skip states have no throughput
  guard anywhere — carried as [R3.1], reframed.

### Independent corroboration of R3-SELF-1/2 (unintentional)

The subagent that reworked `compare.sh` measured case (f) at **90.5 MB/s**
before a mid-session rebuild and **118.5 MB/s** after, and read the second
number as "consistent with the ~40% skip-state-eligibility speedup landing".
Its reading was wrong for the same reason mine was — 90.5 was M2.12's
regression, not a baseline — but its two numbers are an INDEPENDENT
reproduction of the middle two rows of the R3-SELF-1/2 table, taken on a
different harness by someone with no stake in the result. The true baseline is
~158.

Worth keeping as a pattern: both of us anchored on the most recent number
rather than on the historical one, and the R2 published baseline (159.056) was
sitting in the repo the whole time. The compare gate's real contribution is
that it forces that comparison instead of leaving it to whoever remembers.

## Critic panel

All five critics dispatched across the milestone (two on the M2.8 DESIGN before
implementation, three on the M2 close) eventually reported — but ALL of them
after this session had already compiled a "no adversarial coverage" verdict
and pushed it. The reports are excellent and materially changed the outcome:
they produced **one live wrong-behaviour finding in shipped code, one live hole
in a guard I had added hours earlier, and the best unguarded-change finding of
the project so far.** Recorded below by finding, with what was done.

### Critic: engine semantics / trie priority

- **F3** (CONFIRMED, LIVE, NOW FIXED) **`trie_build`'s rule 1 was a ~1.3 MB
  stack budget, and a REGRESSION against this file's own R-2 hardening.** Rule
  1 recursed once per branch ending at a node, so `a|a|...|a` with 9000
  duplicate branches recursed 9000 deep and SEGFAULTED at a 1 MB stack — where
  the pre-trie construction compiled the same pattern at 128 KB, because its
  deep recursion was in tail position and gcc turned it into a jump. pcrec is a
  library; musl's default thread stack is 128 KB. Reproduced exactly as
  reported. FIXED: rule 1 now splits on every accept in ONE pass, so recursion
  is bounded by branch points rather than branch count; `TRIE_MAX_RDEPTH`
  dropped 4096 -> 256 and is now documented AS a stack budget with the measured
  272 B frame behind it (~68 KB). The 9000-branch pattern now compiles at
  64 KB. Guarded by a new `tests/cli` case at a 512 KB limit, validated by
  restoring the recursive form.
- **Latent nit** (FIXED) `bool *used = arena_alloc(...)` is read before any
  explicit write and is correct only because `arena_alloc` zeroes. Now
  commented as a load-bearing dependency.
- **F2 correction** (FIXED) My comment justified the contiguous-run rule with
  "the first matching branch in index order wins". That is NOT the invariant
  D3 maintains — lower-priority threads are deliberately kept alive past an
  accept. The real property is stronger (DFS leaf order restricted to any
  simultaneously-matching set must equal index order), and the composition step
  is CONDITIONAL on rule 2 rather than independent of it. Comment corrected in
  both `nfa.c` and D9.
- **F6** (FIXED) "The trie caps alternation chains at node fan-out" is false
  with no shared prefix — root fan-out IS nbr. Corrected in `nfa.c` so nobody
  cites it to justify a state-count bound.
- **F4** (HELD, and a real trap worth keeping) `a(bc)d` parses as
  `CAT(CAT(a,CAT(b,c)),d)`, so a left-spine walk that counted each right child
  as one step would compute a wrong-length key and factor branches sharing no
  prefix — a silent miscompile. `trie_key` checks every collected node is
  A_CLASS, so it correctly rejects `a(bc)d` and accepts `(ab)c`. Easy to
  "optimize" back into a bug.
- **F5** (CONFIRMED, free optimization NOT taken) The reverse machine is built
  with `prune=false` and both priority-sensitive early-outs are gated on
  `prune`, so branch priority is genuinely irrelevant there — it could factor
  UNCONDITIONALLY, with no rule 1 and no disjointness guard, and running the
  guard in reverse forgoes sharing on exactly the patterns with the richest
  suffix sharing. Left undone deliberately: unmeasured, and this checkpoint has
  already shown what happens when a plausible optimization ships unmeasured.
- **F1** CLOSED, found independently on both sides. Three of the critic's
  repros are now in the corpus, including `foo.x|foobar|foo.` (uses `.`, the
  overlap an author is least likely to notice) and `[ab]xy|a[xz]yy|[ab]`
  (proves the guard is NECESSARY, not merely sufficient — "axyy" and "azyy"
  demand opposite group orders, so no fixed ordering could have worked).

### Critic: cost claims

- **Cost model** independently reproduced before my message arrived: state-list
  length 2061.8 vs 31.4 factored, 2.31e9 clo_visit calls, memset 7.4% of
  runtime, Fix A alone ~4%. Same verdict.
- **gcc time** — the critic reads this as REFUTING my "~1.6x" figure, and the
  two readings reconcile: on FLAT patterns pcrec is 14-35x gcc; after the trie
  lands pcrec drops to 0.79 s against gcc's 1.36 s, i.e. gcc becomes 1.7x the
  larger cost. My number described the shipped state and is right for it; the
  critic's describes the pre-trie state. **Consequence taken:** M2.9's
  compile-time budgets measure only pcrec's half, and after the trie that is
  the SMALLER half. Recorded as a gap.
- **Realistic keyword sets** (ACCEPTED, my claim was overstated) Real lists
  save 25-40%, not "large": Public Suffix List 28.8%, English word samples
  28-42%, random 23.7%. Only alphabetically-contiguous slices (70.9%) or
  reversed domains (60.6%) show heavy sharing. This STRENGTHENS the conclusion
  — no realistic 3600-word list drops under the old 20000 cap by factoring —
  and `nfa.c` now carries the corrected figures and their method.
- **Memory** (NOTED, real) Arena RSS is Theta(|DFA| x L): 33 MB / 109 MB /
  309 MB at 1000/2000/3600 flat branches. Raising the NFA cap without the trie
  would convert an honest hard error into a 47-second hang plus an OOM path.
  Both landed together, so the exposure is closed, but the cap is bounded by
  stack and state count and NOT by |DFA| x L.
- **Fix A trap** (already correct in the shipped code, now understood) The
  generation counter is per-build arena memory along with the marks;
  [CORRECTED 2026-08-09, D19 audit: this bullet originally said the counter is
  "file-scope". It is not — `gen` is a member of `Marks` and `Marks marks` is a
  LOCAL of `pcrec_build_dfa`. The hazard below is real and handled; the scope
  claim was wrong, and had it been true it would have been a thread-safety
  defect.]
  the wrap path must clear the CURRENT build's array and `Clo` must be built
  AFTER the wrap check. Survives the forward/reverse pair only because
  `arena_alloc` zeroes and generation 0 is never live.

## Reflection

1. **Every safeguard that caught a regression this session was built during
   the same session.** The pre-existing suite — 554 corpus cases, the python
   oracle, the PCRE2 fuzzer — was green through both regressions, because both
   were behaviour-preserving. That is the third consecutive checkpoint at which
   behaviour-preserving change has been the blind spot, and the argument for
   `tests/codegen/` and the two new ratchets is now empirical rather than
   theoretical.

2. **The lesson that failed was one already written down.** "A single sample
   is not a measurement" appears twice in earlier journal entries. I built the
   interleaving machinery for exactly this in M2.9, and then failed to apply it
   to my own result hours later. Writing a lesson down does not install it; the
   thing that actually caught the error was a *mechanism* (the gate) rather
   than a remembered principle. Prefer building the mechanism.

3. **The most serious defect of the session was invisible to every test
   because it was in the packaging, not the code.** `src/core/` had been
   untracked since M0 and two adversarial reviews missed it. Both reviewed
   what was on disk. Any future review should include "build the artifact a
   stranger would get" as a first-class check — it is now step 5 of the
   process critic's brief for that reason.

4. **Negative results are results, and this checkpoint has two.** M2.10's skip
   eligibility change and D7's computed-goto arbitration both ended in "no,
   and here is the measurement". Both are recorded as such with the numbers
   attached, and M2.10's is protected by a codegen check asserting the state
   stays skip-INELIGIBLE, so the idea cannot be re-landed on plausibility
   alone. A milestone that only records wins is not recording honestly.
