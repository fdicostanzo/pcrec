## Self-audit (findings the main session found on itself, before the panel)

These are recorded first because they are the most important results of this
checkpoint and none of them came from a critic. They came from tooling built
during the same session, which is the one genuinely encouraging fact here.

- **R3-SELF-1** (CONFIRMED, FIXED) **M2.12 shipped a 43% regression.** The
  accept/EOL evaluation was moved after scan avoidance UNCONDITIONALLY. That
  is required under EOL and merely correct without it — and without it, it
  hoists the memchr prefilter above the accept check: `[01]*1[01]{8}` went
  158.4 → 90.8 MB/s (spreads 1.01–1.02x). Now conditional on `eol`.
  *Caught by:* the M2.11 compare gate, one commit after it was written.

- **R3-SELF-2** (CONFIRMED, REVERTED) **M2.10's skip-eligibility change was a
  27% regression recorded as a 40% improvement.** The "before" sample read
  83.7 MB/s where the true value is ~158 — un-interleaved, taken at load 1.69,
  never repeated. Two independent harnesses now agree.
  *Caught by:* generating the M2.11 gate's floors and noticing case (f) at
  118.6 against R2's published 159.1.

- **R3-SELF-3** (CONFIRMED, FIXED) **A false claim reached a commit message,
  D11, and the journal**: "non-EOL output is byte-identical across 8 probe
  patterns" was true of an earlier draft of M2.12 and false of what was
  committed, because the identity check was not re-run after the final change.
  It is true now and re-verified.

- **R3-SELF-4** (CONFIRMED, CORRECTED) **D13's "there is no crossover" between
  computed-goto and table dispatch was wrong.** The crossover is not in DFA
  size but in transition PREDICTABILITY: on predictable input computed goto is
  3.5x FASTER (ratio 0.28), on random input 2.5x slower. The decision stands;
  the stated reason was misleading. Also: the micro-benchmark's absolute
  numbers are untrustworthy (table reads 7234 MB/s at -O1 vs 350 at -O2), so
  only within-level ratios are meaningful.

- **R3-SELF-5** (CONFIRMED, FIXED) **`.gitignore`'s unanchored `core` matched
  the DIRECTORY `src/core/`**, so `arena.c`, `compile.c`, `sb.c` and
  `internal.h` — the entire pipeline driver — were never committed. **A fresh
  clone of the public repo did not build, and had not since M0.** Both R1 and
  R2 missed it because both reviewed the working tree rather than a clean
  clone. Verified fixed: `git clone && make && make test` now passes
  607/41/17.

- **R3-SELF-6** (CONFIRMED, FIXED) **The M2.9 budgets flake on a busy box.**
  Probing whether budgets tight enough to catch a 1.75x regression are also
  tight enough for load to fail them: yes. At 1-minute load 24.4 on 12 cores a
  known-good build measured 8572 MB/s on case (b) against its 12000 floor, and
  per-trial spreads widened from 1.15–1.35x to 1.38–1.53x. The tempting fix —
  loosen the budget — would have undone M2.9 within a week. Instead the suite
  now declines to judge above `LOAD_LIMIT` (default `max(2.0, cores/2)`): a
  miss becomes INCONCLUSIVE, is not counted, and the summary states that the
  run gated nothing. A PASS under load is still a PASS.

- **R3-SELF-7** (CONFIRMED, OPEN) **The codegen checks assert skip-table
  PRESENCE, not COUNT.** Changing `pick_skip_states`' cap from 4 to 1 — a
  plausible "simplification" — leaves all 17 codegen checks green, along with
  the corpus, the python oracle and the fuzzer. `make bench` did report budget
  failures on that build, but that measurement was taken at load >20 and the
  healthy build failed a budget in the same window, so **whether bench
  reliably catches it is UNVERIFIED** and should be re-measured on a quiet
  box. Triage: PLAN — either assert the expected skip-table count for a known
  pattern, or accept and document that skip-state COUNT is unguarded.

### Independent corroboration of R3-SELF-1/2 (unintentional)

The subagent that reworked `compare.sh` measured case (f) at **90.5 MB/s**
before a mid-session rebuild and **118.5 MB/s** after, and read the second
number as "consistent with the ~40% skip-state-eligibility speedup landing".
Its reading was wrong for the same reason mine was — 90.5 was M2.12's
regression, not a baseline — but its two numbers are an INDEPENDENT
reproduction of the middle two rows of the R3-SELF-1/2 table, taken on a
different harness by someone with no stake in the result. The true baseline is
~158.

Worth keeping as a pattern: both of us anchored on the most recent number
rather than on the historical one, and the R2 published baseline (159.056) was
sitting in the repo the whole time. The compare gate's real contribution is
that it forces that comparison instead of leaving it to whoever remembers.

## Critic panel

Three critics were dispatched (engine semantics, claim auditing,
tests/process) with the briefs described above. **None had reported by the
time the session closed**, despite four status requests and an explicit
final call inviting partial findings and a bare PROBED AND HELD list.

Two DESIGN critics had been dispatched earlier, before M2.8 was implemented,
and fared only slightly better. One sent a single summary line — "critical
CONFIRMED break in rule 2 with overlapping classes, plus 5 more findings" —
and then never sent the report. That headline is worth recording because it is
**independent confirmation of the most serious design bug of the milestone**:
the rule-2 overlapping-class break, which I had found separately while
drafting the code and fixed with the pairwise-disjointness guard. Two
independent parties reaching the same conclusion is real evidence that the
guard is load-bearing rather than defensive. The other five findings were
requested repeatedly and never arrived; if any of them apply to the shipped
code, they are still live and unknown. The second design critic sent nothing.

So the honest tally for this checkpoint is: five critics dispatched, one
usable headline, zero full reports.

This is recorded as a process finding rather than glossed over, and it is the
second consecutive checkpoint with a late/absent critic (R2 noted the same for
its process/tests lens). The consequence is concrete: **R3 has no independent
adversarial coverage.** Everything above is self-audit, which is exactly the
kind of evidence D6 exists to supplement, because a self-audit cannot find the
errors whose cause is the author's own model of the code.

Treat the M2 close as REVIEWED-BY-SELF-ONLY. These areas should be attacked
early next session; the first two now have self-run evidence behind them, the
last two have none:

- **The alternation trie's soundness guards (D9)** — PROBED AND HELD by a
  targeted differential sweep against python `re` written after the panel
  failed to report: randomly generated alternations of 2–6 branches over
  overlapping classes (`[ab]`, `[bc]`, `[a-c]`, `[b-d]`), mixed
  eligible/ineligible branches (repeats, nested `(?:...)` alternations to
  depth 2), each wrapped six ways (bare, `(?:...)+`, `(?:...)*`,
  `(?:...){1,2}`, trailing `$`, prefixed group), over 85 subjects including
  newline-bearing and empty ones. **3 seeds × 11,760 = 35,280 cases, 0
  divergences.**
- **startpos != 0** — PROBED AND HELD separately, since `--emit-main` never
  exercises it: every startpos from 0..n for each subject, via a driver
  calling the generated `rx_search` directly. **2 seeds × 6,240 = 12,480
  cases, 0 divergences.**
- **The EOL/non-EOL ordering asymmetry in `emit_unanchored` (D11)** — only the
  27×69 EOL sweep behind it; no adversarial probe of the ASYMMETRY itself.
- **The iterative `clo_visit` rewrite's equivalence to the recursive
  original** — PROBED AND HELD, and by the strongest available test. A build
  with ONLY `clo_visit` reverted to its recursive form was diffed against the
  shipped one over randomly generated patterns covering `^`, `$`, nested
  groups to depth 3, greedy and lazy quantifiers, bounded repeats including
  `{m,}` and lazy `{m,n}?`, and alternations: **2 seeds, 2,294 patterns, 100%
  byte-IDENTICAL emitted C**, plus 706 rejected identically by both. Identical
  codegen means identical DFAs, which is a stronger statement than matching
  behaviour on any finite set of subjects. This was the highest-risk uncovered
  item because it touches the loop-entry/`reent` empty-iteration rule behind
  two past wrong-answer bugs (R2-S1, K1).
- **Whether D13's dispatch micro-benchmark represents the emitter's actual
  computed-goto shape** — NO independent probe.

Process change for R4: dispatch critics EARLIER, at the start of the last
work item rather than after it, and treat "no report" as a blocking condition
for declaring a milestone reviewed rather than a footnote.

## Reflection

1. **Every safeguard that caught a regression this session was built during
   the same session.** The pre-existing suite — 554 corpus cases, the python
   oracle, the PCRE2 fuzzer — was green through both regressions, because both
   were behaviour-preserving. That is the third consecutive checkpoint at which
   behaviour-preserving change has been the blind spot, and the argument for
   `tests/codegen/` and the two new ratchets is now empirical rather than
   theoretical.

2. **The lesson that failed was one already written down.** "A single sample
   is not a measurement" appears twice in earlier journal entries. I built the
   interleaving machinery for exactly this in M2.9, and then failed to apply it
   to my own result hours later. Writing a lesson down does not install it; the
   thing that actually caught the error was a *mechanism* (the gate) rather
   than a remembered principle. Prefer building the mechanism.

3. **The most serious defect of the session was invisible to every test
   because it was in the packaging, not the code.** `src/core/` had been
   untracked since M0 and two adversarial reviews missed it. Both reviewed
   what was on disk. Any future review should include "build the artifact a
   stranger would get" as a first-class check — it is now step 5 of the
   process critic's brief for that reason.

4. **Negative results are results, and this checkpoint has two.** M2.10's skip
   eligibility change and D7's computed-goto arbitration both ended in "no,
   and here is the measurement". Both are recorded as such with the numbers
   attached, and M2.10's is protected by a codegen check asserting the state
   stays skip-INELIGIBLE, so the idea cannot be re-landed on plausibility
   alone. A milestone that only records wins is not recording honestly.
